## Verdict

**Go — but not on any of the delivery niches, and not on the version the client is probably
imagining.** The single biggest reason: with <A$50,000 you cannot buy both a delivery capability
and the working capital to carry it. The arithmetic below shows that a labour-delivery business
consumes roughly **A$8,100–13,900 of working capital per A$100,000 of annualised revenue** at
civil-industry payment behaviour. Buy a ute and a sign kit and your deployable float drops to
~A$11k, which caps you at **A$79,000–136,000 of annual revenue** — below a living wage for two
people, in a market (traffic control) that is actively consolidating and generating insolvencies.

The entry that survives falsification is the one that has **no crew, no plant, no receivable
risk and a mandated buyer**: selling *compliance documents and cultural capability* into the
Aboriginal-participation budget line that TfNSW and the Commonwealth force tier-1 contractors to
carry. Specifically: **Aboriginal Participation Plan / IPP response writing, traffic-control-plan
(TCP/TGS) design, and cultural awareness training** — three products, one buyer, one budget line,
combined 80%+ gross margin, ~A$15k of capital, first revenue inside 60 days. Those fund the
delivery business (traffic control crews or erosion and sediment control) in year two, financed by
**IBA start-up finance up to A$150k with up to 30% as grant** — not by the client's own A$50k.

Do not do it the other way round. Buying a ute first is the failure mode.

## Part 1 — The falsification: what would make this a bad business?

I went looking for the four things that would kill it. Three of the four are real.

### 1.1 Traffic control — the "classic low-capital entry" — is a distressed, consolidating market

This is the assignment's headline candidate and it is the weakest of the serious options.

| Fact | Source | Date |
| --- | --- | --- |

| 9,300 companies entered external administration in the year to 28 Feb 2026, **+76% on the five-year average** | ASIC, via Gadens **[B]** | Feb 2026 |

Read that as an operator: the market leader has 5,500 badged controllers and 50 depots, is buying
its competitors, and the sector is still generating insolvencies. A three-person entrant is not

finding here. "Too many competitors, all bleeding" is.**

### 1.2 The Indigenous participation mandate buys a *name on a form*, and the price of that is set by competition

The demand is real and legislated. It is also, precisely because it is a compliance quantity rather
than a capability purchase, **highly price-competitive**.

| Mandate | What it forces | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **TfNSW**: minimum **1.5% Aboriginal participation on all contracts ≥ A$7.5m** — met by subcontracting to Aboriginal businesses, employing Aboriginal people, *or* spending on education/training/capability building | Creates a budget line on every major NSW road/rail package | Transport for NSW **[A]** |
| **TfNSW**: 3% of goods-and-services contracts to Aboriginal-owned suppliers (target, not floor) | Panel and direct-buy opportunity | Transport for NSW **[A]** |
| **Commonwealth IPP**: Mandatory Minimum Requirements on contracts **≥ A$7.5m** in specified industries (construction included) | Same forcing function federally | NIAA IPP Guidelines, 12 May 2026 **[A]** |
| **Commonwealth IPP Mandatory Set-Aside**: procurements **A$80k–A$200k** (GST inc.) wholly delivered in Australia, and *all* values in remote Australia, must offer Indigenous business first opportunity | A protected slice of small contracts | NIAA **[A]** |
| **Commonwealth Exemption 16 (CPRs)**: buyers may procure **directly from an Indigenous SME at any value**, value-for-money via simple quote | No open tender required — the fastest legitimate door in Australian procurement | NIAA / Dept Finance **[A]** |

**Note the wording of the TfNSW 1.5%: "or spending on education, training or capability building."**
That is the specific clause that makes cultural awareness training and capability spend a
*substitute* for subcontracting. A tier-1 short of its 1.5% at month 20 of a project will buy the
cheapest, fastest-to-invoice compliant thing. A training day and a participation plan close a gap
faster than mobilising a traffic crew. **That is the wedge.**

### 1.3 The ownership test tightened on 1 July 2026 — and the obvious shortcut is now the biggest risk

| Fact | Source | Date |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IPP definition moved from **50% Indigenous owned** to **51% Indigenous owned *or controlled***, effective **1 July 2026** | NIAA / Sparke Helmore **[A]** | 1 Jul 2026 |
| 2026 research finds the reform "doesn't go far enough": black cladding also covers symbolic ownership, Indigenous owners excluded from decision-making, unfair financial returns, and **"questionable sub-contracting arrangements"** | UQ *Indigenous Journal of Business*, The Conversation **[B]** | 2026 |
| Supply Nation registration is **free**; requires minimum Indigenous ownership, ABN, financial viability | Supply Nation **[A]** | 2026 |

The natural move for a business with no track record is to JV with an established non-Indigenous
contractor and borrow their plant, insurance and history. **That is exactly the structure now under
active scrutiny.** If it goes wrong the client does not lose a contract; it loses the only asset it
has, which is its standing. Any JV must survive a control test, not just an ownership test.

The client asked which niches avoid the trap. Here is the arithmetic, built from sourced inputs.

**The calculation** `[ESTIMATE — arithmetic shown, attack the inputs]`

Labour model: direct cost = 71% of revenue. Wages paid weekly (avg 3.5 days after work performed).
Invoice raised at month end (avg 15 days after work performed). Cash gap = days from wage outlay to
cash receipt. Working capital = annual direct cost × gap ÷ 365.

| Scenario | Cash gap | **WC per A$100k annualised revenue** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tier-1 subcontract, paid on 30-day contract terms | 41.5 days | **A$8,073** |
| Tier-1 subcontract, real-world 60-days-late behaviour | 71.5 days | **A$13,908** |
| Add 5% retention held 12 months | — | **+A$5,000** |
| **Direct to NSW Government**, 5 business days, monthly invoicing | 18 days | **A$3,501** |
| **Direct to NSW Government**, 5 business days, **weekly** invoicing | 10.5 days | **A$2,042** |
| Training / fixed-fee consulting, invoice on delivery, 30-day terms | 33 days | **A$1,808** |
| Fixed-fee consulting **with 50% deposit** | negative | **~A$0 or negative** |

**Now the ceiling that decides the strategy:**

| Capital deployed to working capital | At contract terms | At real-world terms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A$35,000 (labour-only, hire all equipment) | A$434,000 revenue | **A$252,000** revenue |
| A$11,000 (after buying a used ute + sign kit ~A$24k) | A$136,000 revenue | **A$79,000** revenue |

**Buying delivery assets cuts your revenue ceiling by roughly two thirds.** That single line is the
strategic argument of this memo. And note the direct-to-government column: invoicing NSW Government
weekly instead of subcontracting to a tier-1 monthly reduces working capital intensity **~7x**.
Channel choice, not cost control, is the lever.

### G. Cultural heritage monitoring  — **RANK 7, structurally gated**

The demand thesis is right; the access thesis is probably wrong for this client.

| Fact | Source |
| --- | --- |

**The structural problem:** in both NSW and Victoria the work is routed by statute to the Traditional

**Rate evidence is thin:** a Victorian 2015 schedule quoted A$150/hr beyond an 8-hour day **[C]**;
Seek shows a temporary heritage role at **A$67–72/hr + super** and a Broome heritage officer at
**A$80,000–100,000** **[A-ish]**. That is a wage, not a business margin.

**Where the actual business is:** not monitoring, but **coordinating** it — the proponent-side
logistics of consultation, scheduling monitors, AHIMS searches, RAP registration administration.
That is billable consulting with no Country-authority problem. Unpriced; **not found**.

### H. White-collar labour supply to tier-1s  — **NO-GO**

Three independent walls:

1. **Licensing with a financial viability test.** Queensland requires demonstrated financial
   viability as part of the fit-and-proper test. Victoria: application A$1,815.48 tier 1 (turnover
   <A$2m) + annual A$1,260.75. ACT: A$3,461 (2026–27). SA: A$973 individual / A$2,212 company, **all
   industries since 29 January 2026**, allow ≥6 weeks. (Sources: Labour Hire Authority Vic, WorkSafe
   ACT, Labour Hire Licensing QLD, xrecruiter **[A]/[C]**.) **NSW and WA status: not found.** A
   barely-trading company with no accounts is exactly what a financial viability test is designed to
   stop.
2. **Margin.** "Honest margins sit between 15 and 25 per cent of the loaded cost"; construction
   labour hire 25–35% on direct employment cost (Wise Recruitments, Harrison Barratt **[C]**).
3. **Working capital.** A$8,073–13,908 per A$100k of revenue, and white-collar contractors are
   expensive per head — one A$1,000/day engineer is A$220,000/yr of revenue needing A$18k–31k of
   float, alone.

### I. Drone / UAV survey  — **NO-GO for year one**

RePL **A$1,800–A$3,500** (some from A$1,799 incl. CASA fees); **ReOC from A$2,200+GST to A$3,750**;
5 days training plus 5 logged flight hours; commercial work requires an ReOC or operating under
someone else's (ACE Aviation, Rotate, UASNT **[C]**). **Australian day rates: not found.** The flight
is the commodity; the value is in the photogrammetry processing and the surveyor's certification —
which the incumbent survey firms already own, along with the drones. Nothing here creates
preferential Indigenous demand.

### J. Plant hire brokerage  — **NO-GO on available evidence**

**Broker economics: not found.** The structural objection is fatal on its face: a broker pays the
plant owner on the owner's terms and collects on the client's — you take the working capital risk of
plant hire with none of the asset margin. iSeekPlant already occupies the marketplace position.

### K. Site services (fencing/hoarding hire, amenities, waste, cleaning, dust suppression)  — **NO-GO**

Hire businesses are asset businesses; asset businesses need capital the client does not have.
Cleaning is low-margin labour with the same receivable problem and no Indigenous demand uplift.
**AU rates: not found** for any of these in a civil context.

## Part 4 — The gap, stated precisely

**Who:** the Aboriginal/Indigenous Participation Manager (or bid manager) at a tier-1 or tier-2 civil
contractor holding a TfNSW package **≥A$7.5m** or a Commonwealth construction contract **≥A$7.5m**.

**What they can't do today:** demonstrate and evidence 1.5% Aboriginal participation with a supplier
who can (a) write the plan credibly, (b) deliver the training that counts toward it, and (c) later
supply the labour it commits to — as one accountable Indigenous-owned counterparty. Today they
assemble that from three unrelated vendors, and the plan is usually written by a non-Indigenous bid
consultant, which is the part that reads badly to an evaluator.

**What it costs them:** failure to meet participation commitments is a contractual non-conformance on
the package, surfaced in performance reporting that feeds prequalification renewal. The budget is
already priced into the tender — this is not new spend, it is spend looking for a compliant home.

**Why this is the strategy, not three separate ideas:** one buyer, one budget line, three products,
escalating capital intensity. Products 1 and 3 have ~zero working capital and generate the
relationships and the track record that products 5 and 6 require. **Sell the paper first, buy the ute
second.**

---

## Part 5 — Kill shots, ranked by probability

1. **The client buys delivery capability before it buys a customer.** (High.) A ute and a sign kit
   drop deployable working capital from ~A$35k to ~A$11k, which drops the revenue ceiling from
   A$252k to **A$79k at real-world payment behaviour.** This is the most likely way the A$50k dies,
   and it will feel like progress while it happens.
2. **Tier-1s already have an Indigenous traffic management supplier.** (High — **unverified, and it
   is the single most important unknown in this memo.**) With Altus at 5,500 heads consolidating the
   sector, the majors have had years to lock in participation partners. If 6+ of 10 tier-1s say
   "we're covered", the delivery thesis is dead and only the consulting thesis survives.
3. **Working capital / one late tier-1.** (Medium-high.) 7.15% of construction invoices are >60 days
   overdue and 92% of construction firms carried overdue invoices in the last 12 months. NSW SOP Act
   caps subcontractor terms at 20 business days and voids longer terms — but statutory rights are
   worth what your ability to enforce them is worth, and a startup does not adjudicate against its
   only customer. Mitigations that actually work: **register on buy.nsw for 5-business-day payment**,
   Peppol eInvoicing for Commonwealth 5-day terms, elect **cash-basis GST** (available under A$10m
   turnover), invoice weekly, refuse retentions on labour-only scopes.
4. **Black cladding exposure via the obvious JV shortcut.** (Medium.) The 51% owned-**or-controlled**
   test took effect 1 July 2026 and researchers are already pushing beyond ownership to control,
   returns and "questionable sub-contracting arrangements". A structure that borrows a
   non-Indigenous partner's track record is the structure under the microscope.
5. **The consulting businesses are the founder's calendar.** (Medium — certain, if unaddressed.)
   Ranks 1–4 have no leverage and no asset. A lost month is a lost quarter, and there is no "large
   outcome" without productising: templates, a recurring participation-reporting subscription, or
   a trained bench of writers/facilitators. Plan the productisation from month one or accept a
   lifestyle consultancy.

## Part 6 — Cheapest next test (next 30 days, <A$1,500 total)

**Test C — the one that decides everything. Do this first. (Cost: A$0, ~3 days of calls.)**
Ring the Aboriginal/Indigenous Participation Manager at **10 named tier-1/tier-2 civil contractors on
current NSW packages ≥A$7.5m**. Three questions: (i) are you currently short of your 1.5%? (ii) who
writes your Aboriginal Participation Plan? (iii) what do you pay per hour for a traffic control crew?
**Stop signal:** if more than 6 of 10 say they already have an Indigenous traffic management supplier
*and* an in-house/retained plan writer, abandon both the delivery and the writing thesis and re-scope
to cultural awareness training only.

**Test A — bid writing demand. (Cost: A$0.)**
Approach **20 tier-2/3 civil contractors** currently tendering TfNSW or council work. Offer a fixed
**A$2,500** to write the Aboriginal Participation Plan section, **50% deposit on engagement**.
**Stop signal:** fewer than **2 of 20** pay a deposit within 30 days. (Two conversions from twenty
approaches supports the 25-engagement year-one model at a sustainable call volume.)

**Test B — TCP design repeat rate. (Cost: ~A$600 ads + ~A$900 credential enquiry.)**
Run 30 days of Google Ads on "traffic control plan [city]" / "traffic guidance scheme". Count
qualified enquiries, quoted value, and — critically — ask every enquirer **how many plans they buy a
year**. **Stop signal:** fewer than **15 enquiries**, or CPA above **A$120**, or median stated
purchase frequency below **4 plans/year** (below that, CAC never amortises).

**Test D — verify the two load-bearing second-hand claims. (Cost: A$0, 1 hour.)**
Open, with your own eyes, (i) the TfNSW *Registration Scheme for Construction Industry Contractors
Guidelines and Conditions* PDF and confirm Category G carries **no financial capacity assessment**;
(ii) the TfNSW PWZTMP course fee and next available date. Both are stated here from search snippets,
not from the page. If (i) is wrong, rank 5 and 6 get materially harder.

**And one funding action, not a test:** apply to **Indigenous Business Australia**. Start-Up Finance
Package: loans **up to A$150,000**, **up to 30% awarded as grant**, term to 7 years, low interest.
Working Capital Assistance: **up to A$200,000** (capped grant + loan), 5-year term, **no repayments
for the first 12 months**, preferential rates (IBA **[A]**). The A$50k constraint is real for the
first six months and largely artificial after that — but IBA will want to see trading history, which
is exactly what ranks 1–3 produce cheapest.
