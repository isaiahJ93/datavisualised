# Boxing / Muay Thai bag gantry — structural design report

**Structure:** freestanding 15 m hot-dip galvanised steel gantry, 7 hangers (6 or 7 bags), fixed-base columns on cast-in anchors and reinforced concrete pad footings.
**Design life:** 50 years structure; 5 years replaceable hanging hardware.
**Status:** Rev P1, preliminary design. This package is complete enough to price, to detail for fabrication, and to hand to a chartered structural engineer for certification. It is not a certified design and must not be built from until an engineer registered in the relevant jurisdiction has reviewed it against site conditions and signed it off. Items the certifier must confirm are listed in Section 15.

Calculations: `calcs/gantry_calcs.py` (pure Python, re-runnable). Check table: `calcs/RESULTS.md`. Drawings: `drawings/GA-01`, `DET-01`, `DET-02`, `DET-03` (SVG, regenerated by `drawings/make_drawings.py`).

## 3. Layout and geometry

- **Overall length 15.0 m** between end column centrelines. Column bays 4.5 / 6.0 / 4.5 m so that the two intermediate columns fall midway between bags, 1.0 m from the adjacent hanger centrelines and 1.5 m from the end hangers. Columns are padded; pair the shorter boxing bags with the hangers next to columns and use the three mid-bay hangers for the long Muay Thai bags.
- **Seven hangers at 2.0 m centres.** For six bags use hangers 1-3 and 5-7 and put a double-end or speed bag at the 7.5 m position.
- **Beam soffit 3.0 m.** A 1.8 m Muay Thai bag with its base 0.3 m off the floor hangs from a 0.7 m shackle/swivel/chain set; a 1.2-1.5 m boxing bag uses a 0.6-0.9 m set. Roof clearance to underside of purlins or services: 3.6 m minimum.
- **Clear zone.** Keep 2.0 m clear each side of the bag line and 1.5 m beyond the end bags. The 1.0 m radius dashed circles on GA-01 are the swing envelope of a hard-hit bag.
- **Freestanding.** Nothing is tied to walls or roof. Do not brace the gantry to the building without a separate check: the dynamic loads would be transferred into the wall.

## 4. Actions

### 4.1 Bag actions (per hanger, characteristic)

| Action | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Rated bag mass | 100 kg (W = 0.98 kN) | Posted SWL. Covers 6 ft Muay Thai bags (45-70 kg) and 200 lb heavy bags with margin |
| Vertical dynamic Q_v | 3.92 kN = 4.0 × W | Envelope of: pendulum tension after a hard strike (≈ 1.2 W), snatch when a bag is lifted and dropped or a person jumps on a bag (3-5 W). A 60 kg bag kicked with an 8 kN, 15 ms impulse swings to ≈ 24° and peaks at 1.2 W; the 4 W envelope is deliberately for abuse, not normal use |
| Horizontal Q_h | 2.0 kN in any direction | ≈ 2 W. Rigid-body impulse analysis of a strike at mid-height gives a pivot impulse of about 0.25 × the strike impulse (≈ 2 kN peak); the pendulum horizontal component peaks at ≈ 0.5 W. 2.0 kN is an upper envelope |
| Hardware self-weight | 0.15 kN | Lug plate, shackle, swivel, chains |
| Simultaneity | All 7 hangers loaded with Q_v and Q_h in the same direction at once | Conservative for the frame and footings (seven people kicking the same way on the same instant); cheap to design for and it is what keeps a freestanding frame from ever walking or rocking |

### 4.2 Other actions

- **Self-weight:** beam 0.65 kN/m, plates and bolts as point loads, columns 89 kg/m.
- **Temperature:** ±20 K from erection temperature (unconditioned shed). The beam is axially rigid relative to the columns, so the columns are forced to follow the beam's free expansion about its centre; end columns pick up 3.94 kN (12.0 kN·m at the base), intermediate columns 1.58 kN (4.8 kN·m). Applied with γ = 1.0 in the strength combinations. Excluded from footing stability because it is strain-controlled and relaxes if the footing rotates.
- **Notional horizontal load and wind:** internal structure; the bag horizontal loads are far larger than 0.2 % of the vertical load. Not governing.
- **Fatigue spectrum:** ΔF_v = 1.0 kN and ΔF_h = 1.0 kN per strike at one hanger, 2 × 10⁸ cycles per hanger over 50 years (7 bags × 3 h/day × 60 strikes/min). The column base is checked for two adjacent bags striking together (ΔH = 2 kN).

### 4.3 Combinations (AS/NZS 1170.0)

| Case | Combination | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| U1 | 1.2 G + 1.5 Q_v | Beam bending, column axial |
| U2 | 1.2 G + 1.5 (Q_v + Q_h transverse) | Column and base, transverse |
| U3 | 1.2 G + 1.5 (Q_v + Q_h longitudinal) + T | Column and base, longitudinal; beam axial |
| U4 | 1.2 G + 1.5 (Q_v + 0.71 Q_h both directions) + T | Biaxial base plate |
| U5 | 0.9 G + 1.0 W_static + 1.5 Q_h (+ T) | Anchor tension (minimum axial with maximum moment) |
| Stability | 0.9 G stabilising vs 1.5 Q_h destabilising, about the pad toe | Pad footing overturning |
| SLS | G + Q (short-term) | Deflection, sway, bearing pressure |

## 5. Analysis

The beam is a continuous cubic-element beam on four supports (rigid for vertical loads; column-tip springs of k = 3EI/L³ = 2.19 MN/m for transverse loads). Columns are fixed-base cantilevers with L_c = 3.053 m from the top of the base plate to the beam centroid. Longitudinal loads are shared equally by the four equal columns through the axially rigid beam.

## 6. Members

**Beam 250 × 250 × 9.0 SHS C350L0.** Compact (flange slenderness 27 < 30). φM_s = 236 kN·m; biaxial interaction 0.02; shear 0.02; torsion 0.01. The beam is sized for lateral stiffness and torsional stiffness (bags pull sideways from a lug 200 mm below the centroid), for a flat 250 mm bottom face to weld the hanger plates onto, and for vibration. An I-section of similar mass has a lateral frequency below 5 Hz and would feel alive under kicks; the SHS is what makes the frame feel dead.

**Columns 250 × 250 × 12.5 SHS C350L0.** φN_s = 3 572 kN, φN_c = 2 354 kN (fixed-free, k_e = 2.2), φM_s = 313 kN·m. Section interaction 0.09, member 0.09, shear 0.01. The thicker wall is for sway stiffness: it lifts the rigid-base sway frequency from 9.3 Hz (9.0 wall) to 9.8 Hz and gives 7.7 Hz with base and soil flexibility. Fatigue at the base weld toe (category 36, tube-to-plate fillet, t > 8 mm) is at 0.40 of the infinite-life limit under a two-bag strike.

## 7. Connections

**Beam-to-column (DET-03).** Cap plate 420 × 420 × 20 shop welded to the column (8 mm fillet all round); shoe plate 420 × 420 × 16 shop welded under the beam; 4 × M20 8.8/TB at 340 gauge, 45 mm outside the tube faces. Designed for the vertical reaction (23 kN ULS), the larger of the transverse or longitudinal shear (9.2 kN), beam torsion (1.8 kN·m) and a nominal 10 kN·m joint moment for robustness. Bolt combined ratio 0.01; plate bending 0.21; cap-plate weld 0.09; beam web bearing 0.01. Bolts are outside the closed sections so nothing needs blind fasteners, and every bolt is inspectable.

**Splices (DET-03).** Two 420 × 420 × 20 flange plates, 8 × M20 8.8/TB. The splices sit 0.5 m into the 6.0 m bay where M_y = 5.6 kN·m and M_z ≈ 0 at ULS. Bolt combined ratio < 0.01; plate bending 0.06; weld 0.05. The eight-bolt pattern gives the same capacity about both axes.

**Hangers (DET-02).** 200 × 300 × 16 plate, 8 mm fillet all round, with a 20 mm × 100 mm lug (Ø22 hole, R50 end) welded both sides. The plate is 200 mm wide on a 250 mm beam so the weld lines sit within 16 mm of the beam webs and the beam's bottom wall is never in significant local bending (0.05). Lug: net tension 0.01, out-of-plane bending 0.06, pin bearing 0.03, tear-out 0.02; welds ≤ 0.06. Rated gear: 3.25 t WLL bolt-type bow shackle (0.21 of WLL at ULS) and 2 t WLL bearing swivel (0.34). Use bolt-type shackles with a nut and split pin; screw pins unwind under a rotating bag. The lug plane is parallel to the beam so the shackle bow can swing across the beam.

Welded rather than through-bolted hangers were chosen deliberately: through-bolts tensioned across a 9 mm tube wall yield the wall locally and then loosen, while a welded plate on a lightly stressed beam has enormous fatigue margin (beam wall stress range at the weld toe is 0.002 MPa against a 25.8 MPa limit).

## 8. Base plates (DET-01)

550 × 550 × 32 Grade 350 (f_y = 340 MPa), unstiffened, 10 mm fillet weld all round to the column, 4 × Ø30 holes at 450 gauge (100 mm from the column face, 50 mm edge distance).

Governing case is U5 at an end column: N* = 4.1 kN with M* = 28.1 kN·m (longitudinal bag load plus thermal restraint). Large-eccentricity analysis with the bearing block at the plate edge limited to 27 MPa (grout, AS 3600 12.6): block length 4 mm, compression 58 kN, tension in the far anchor pair 54.1 kN (27.0 kN per bolt).

| Check | Utilisation |
|---|---|
| Plate bending, tension side (cantilever 100 mm, effective width 2 m per corner bolt) | 0.17 (t_req 13.3 mm) |
| Plate bending, compression side | 0.20 |
| Shear by friction on the compression block, μ = 0.4 | 0.39 (anchors in bearing as backup) |
| Column-to-plate 10 mm fillet weld | 0.21 |

The plate is deliberately thick for its demand. A thick plate gives a prying-free joint, keeps the anchor stress range under cyclic load tiny, and provides the base rotational stiffness (≈ 78 MN·m/rad) assumed in the sway-frequency check. Do not reduce it below 25 mm without re-running the frequency check.

Grout: 40 mm bed of non-shrink cementitious grout (≥ 50 MPa, e.g. a Class C precision grout), full contact, placed after the frame is plumbed on levelling nuts. Anchor nuts are snugged plus half a turn after the grout has cured 3 days, then lock-nutted.

## 9. Anchors (AS 5216)

4 × M24 Grade 8.8 HDG threaded rod × 650 long per column, cast-in through a plywood template, 100 × 100 × 16 anchor plate with a double nut below and single nut above (never weld to Grade 8.8 rod), h_ef = 450 mm, 150 mm projection above concrete, 65 × 65 × 8 plate washers, nut plus lock nut (or wedge-lock washers).

| Mode | Demand | Design capacity | Utilisation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steel tension, per anchor | 27.0 kN | 188 kN (A_s 353 mm², f_uk 800, γ_Ms 1.5) | 0.14 |
| Concrete cone, 2-anchor tension group | 54.1 kN | 427 kN (N⁰_Rk,c = 8.9 √32 × 450^1.5 = 481 kN, A_c/A⁰_c = 1.33, ψ_s = 1.0, γ_Mc 1.5) | 0.13 |
| Pull-out, head bearing | 27.0 kN | 1 530 kN | 0.02 |
| Steel shear per anchor (friction ignored) | 2.3 kN | 113 kN | 0.02 |
| Concrete pry-out | 9.2 kN | 1 710 kN | 0.01 |
| Fatigue, bolt in tension (category 50, pretensioned) | 4.9 MPa | 25.8 MPa | 0.19 |

Edge distance to the pad face is 675 mm, equal to c_cr,N = 1.5 h_ef, so the full cone develops with no edge reduction. Embedment plus anchor plate plus bottom cover is 541 mm inside the 600 mm pad. Splitting is controlled by the hairpins and the top and bottom mesh.

**Cast-in was chosen over post-installed anchors** because the frame sees 10⁸-10⁹ load cycles: headed cast-in anchors do not depend on adhesive bond or expansion force for their long-term capacity, and they let the anchor plate sit just above the bottom mesh so the whole pad is mobilised.

## 10. Pad footings and concrete

Four pads 1 800 × 1 800 × 600 mm, top flush with FFL, on 50 mm N20 blinding, concrete N32 (f'c = 32 MPa, 20 mm aggregate, 80 mm slump, 7 days curing), cover 50 mm (75 mm to blinding), N16 @ 200 each way top and bottom, 4 × N16 U-bar hairpins with 1 000 mm legs around each anchor group, N12 @ 300 dowels 450 mm each side into the surrounding slab (epoxied into an existing slab, cast in for a new slab).

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Overturning, intermediate column, transverse: 1.5 Q_h at 3.72 m above the pad base vs 0.9 (pad + steel) × 0.9 m | 31.1 / 43.3 kN·m = 0.72 |
| Overturning, end column, longitudinal | 19.6 / 40.3 kN·m = 0.49 |
| Bearing pressure (SLS, e = 0.37 m) | 39 kPa maximum against an assumed 100 kPa allowable |
| Pad bending, cantilever from the plate edge | 21.7 / 378 kN·m = 0.06 |
| Minimum flexural reinforcement (AS 3600 8.1.6.1, α_b = 0.20) | 976 / 1 005 mm²/m = 0.97 |
| One-way shear (k_v = 0.15, no shear reinforcement) | 0.03 |

The pads are sized by overturning of a freestanding frame with every bag kicked the same way at once, not by strength. A 1.5 m pad only just balances that case; 1.8 m gives a 1.4 margin on the factored check (about 2.3 on characteristic loads) before any help from the slab dowels. Why N32 rather than N25: anchor cone capacity scales with √f'c and durability against the sweat and wash-down of a gym floor is better; N40 adds only 12 % capacity that is not needed.

**Existing slab option.** A typical 100-150 mm gym slab cannot carry a moment base with 450 mm anchors and does not have the local mass to resist overturning. If pads cannot be cut in, the only acceptable alternative is a continuous reinforced slab at least 300 mm thick and 25 MPa (cored and tested, three cores minimum) with post-installed M24 chemical anchors at 250 mm embedment, and that requires a site-specific check by the certifying engineer. It is not covered by these calculations.

## 12. Fabrication and protection

- Steel: SHS to AS 1163 C350L0 (impact-tested grade), plate to AS/NZS 3678-350. Mill certificates required.
- All welding in the shop before galvanising; SP category to AS/NZS 1554.1 by qualified welders; 100 % visual, 10 % magnetic-particle on the column-to-base-plate and hanger welds. Grind lug hole edges and remove all sharp corners and weld spatter (fatigue and galvanising).
- Vent and drain holes for hot-dip galvanising: minimum 2 × Ø25 at each end of every closed member at diagonally opposite corners, plus holes in end plates where the galvaniser requires. Confirm with the galvaniser before cutting.
- Hot-dip galvanise all steel to AS/NZS 4680 after fabrication (minimum average 85 µm on ≥ 6 mm steel). Bolts, nuts and washers HDG to AS 1214 with tapped-oversize nuts. Site-damaged zinc repaired with two coats of zinc-rich epoxy to AS/NZS 4680 Section 8.
- Match-drill splice and cap/shoe plates in pairs before galvanising. Bolt lengths chosen so at least one full thread projects beyond the nut.
- Corrosion: galvanising alone is adequate for an interior gym for 50+ years. In a coastal or open-sided shed add a two-coat epoxy/polyurethane duplex system over the galvanising and use stainless (A4-70) hanger shackles and swivels.

## 13. Erection, grouting and commissioning

1. Saw-cut and remove 1.9 m squares of existing slab at the four column positions (or form pads with the new slab). Excavate, compact, place 50 mm blinding.
2. Fix reinforcement and the anchor cages in plywood templates; check gauge (450 ± 2 mm) and projection (150 ± 5 mm). Pour N32, cure 7 days, no load for 14 days.
3. Erect columns on levelling nuts; plumb to ≤ H/500; land the three beam lengths, bolt shoe plates and splices snug, align the beam level to ± 5 mm and straight to ± 5 mm, then tension all M20 bolts by turn-of-nut and mark them.
4. Grout the base plates (40 mm, full contact, formed edges); cure 3 days; snug the anchor nuts plus half a turn; fit lock nuts.
5. Fit hangers: bolt-type shackle (nut, split pin), swivel, then the bag chain set. Fit column padding.
6. Proof-load every hanger with 300 kg (3.0 kN) static for 10 minutes; inspect welds and bolts; record. Fix the SWL signage: **"Max bag 100 kg per hanger. No climbing on frame."**

## 14. Inspection and maintenance for a 50-year life

| Interval | Action |
|---|---|
| Monthly (gym staff) | Visual: shackle pins secured, swivels turning freely, chains not worn, no loose nuts, padding intact |
| 6-monthly | Torque-check hanger shackle bolts; check for lug hole wear (replace shackle at 10 % pin-hole wear); check base-plate nuts and grout |
| Annually | Torque-check all M20 bolts against the erection marks; inspect galvanising, especially at the base; check footings for cracking or movement |
| 5-yearly | Replace shackles and swivels regardless of condition; structural engineer's inspection with magnetic-particle test of the column base welds and hanger welds |
| After any incident | Frame struck by equipment, bag dropped from height, or visible movement: engineer's inspection before use |

## 15. Assumptions and items for the certifying engineer

1. Bag mass ≤ 100 kg per hanger and no other equipment (ropes, rings, pull-up bars) attached to the frame. If suspension trainers or rings are wanted, add dedicated lugs and re-check the beam locally.
2. Interior, unconditioned building; ±20 K temperature range from erection; no wind, snow or seismic actions on the frame (an internal structure of this mass in a low-seismicity site is governed by the bag loads; the certifier should confirm the site hazard).
3. Allowable bearing pressure ≥ 100 kPa and soil shear modulus ≥ 15 MPa under the pads (firm clay, medium-dense sand or better); confirm by a site inspection or test pit. Softer ground needs larger pads or a ground beam.
4. The pads are dowelled to the surrounding slab; the stability check is satisfied without that contribution.
5. Existing slab thickness and grade at the four locations to be confirmed before saw-cutting (services, post-tensioning, vapour barrier).
6. Fabricator to shop-detail from these drawings; the certifier to review shop drawings, weld procedure specifications and galvaniser's vent hole layout.
7. The dynamic load model (4 W vertical, 2 W horizontal, all bags simultaneous) is an envelope derived from first principles and industry practice, not from measurements on this frame. The certifier may adjust it; the frame has large margin either way, the footings have a 1.4 margin on the factored overturning check.
8. Section properties were computed for cold-formed corners (r_o = 2.5 t); manufacturer's tabulated values may differ by about 1-2 %.
