## VERDICT

**Go — on defence, and only on the unclassified, labour-and-services end of it. No-go on
health/care and financial services for at least 24 months.**

The single biggest reason: **Defence is the one buyer where the Indigenous lever is already
converting money at scale, and where a brand-new supply chain is being stood up right now.**
Defence has awarded **over $5bn to First Nations businesses under the IPP — roughly half of the
entire ~$10bn Commonwealth IPP spend to date, across 25,100+ contracts to 1,180+ businesses**
[A, defence.gov.au, 9 Oct 2024]. No other portfolio is close. And in **September 2025 Defence
re-awarded its entire national base-services estate — $7.35bn to Downer, Ventia and Service
Stream, contracts commencing February 2026** [A, fmmedia.com.au / Capital Brief, Sep 2025].
Those three primes are building their subcontractor supply chains **now**, under contractual
Australian Industry Capability obligations that explicitly score SME and Indigenous
participation [A, defence.gov.au AIC Industry Guide]. That is a genuine, dated, time-limited
window, and it does not require DISP, does not require a security clearance, and does not
require capital you do not have.

**The three things that would make this a bad business, and what I found:**

- *"The credential gate eats the $50k before revenue arrives."* — **True if you chase the wrong
  door.** DISP + Essential Eight ML2 + clearances is a **6–12 month, $20k–50k+** project [A/B mix]
  with no revenue attached to it. **Do not start there.** The base-services and Exemption 16
  routes need none of it.
- *"The Indigenous set-aside is already carved up."* — **Substantially true, and this is the most
  important falsifying finding in the memo.** **18 firms took 50% ($3.5bn) of the first $7bn of
  federal Indigenous procurement (2015-16 to 2022-23); 11 businesses took 50% of the contracts by
  count; spend is concentrated in capital cities, led by Canberra** [A, Australian Journal of
  Public Administration via The Conversation / ANU, Feb 2026]. You are not competing against
  non-Indigenous firms. You are competing against mature Indigenous firms with ten years of past
  performance. Plan for the tail of that distribution, not the mean.
- *"You are about to become ineligible."* — **Possibly true, and it is urgent.** From
  **1 July 2026 the IPP definition of an Indigenous business moved from 50% to 51% Indigenous
  owned or controlled** [A, NIAA/MinterEllison/Sparke Helmore, announced Feb 2025, IPP Guidelines
  reissued 12 May 2026]. If your business is exactly 50%, **you are already outside the policy as
  at today's date.** Check this before anything else in this memo.

## PART 0 — DO THIS BEFORE YOU READ FURTHER

Three checks, all free or near-free, all of which can invalidate the rest of the plan:

1. **Are you 51%+ Indigenous owned *and* controlled?** The IPP threshold changed from 50% to 51%
   **effective 1 July 2026** [A]. ORIC-registered corporations are exempt from the 51% test and
   qualify on CATSI Act Indigeneity grounds instead [A]. If you sit at 50%, you have been
   ineligible for two months and every hour spent on defence pipeline is wasted until the cap
   table is fixed.
2. **Are you Supply Nation *Registered* or *Certified*?** These are different products.
   Registered has historically been the lower bar; Certified is the one that requires majority
   Indigenous ownership *and* management *and* control, and it is Certified that most corporate
   and prime supplier-diversity programs ask for by name [B — verify the current definitions and
   fee schedule directly with Supply Nation; **their fee for certification is "not found" in this
   research** and you should get it in writing].
3. **Do you have an ABN with 2+ years of lodged BAS, and can you produce a Statement of Tax
   Record?** An STR is a stated minimum content requirement for Commonwealth ICT panel
   applications [A, BuyICT/AusTender] and is routinely required elsewhere. A near-dormant entity
   can fail this before anyone reads your capability statement.

### 1.2 DISP — the gate, priced honestly

**[A] There is no application fee.** That is the headline everyone repeats and it is misleading.
The cost is the readiness work.

- **Four levels** [A, defence.gov.au + dispmembership.com.au + cybernion]: **Entry Level**
  (sensitive-but-unclassified, baseline practices), **Level 1** (PROTECTED — facility and ICT
  accreditation, cleared personnel, formal risk management), **Level 2** (SECRET), **Level 3**
  (TOP SECRET).
- **Every level, including Entry, requires ASD Essential Eight at Maturity Level 2** across the
  ICT systems used to correspond with Defence [A, multiple consultancy sources]. This is the real
  bill. Application control and patching at ML2 on a micro-business fleet is not a checkbox.
- **You must nominate a Chief Security Officer and a Security Officer, and stand up a
  `DISP@yourentity` mailbox** [A, defence.gov.au factsheet]. **You have no staff.** Two named
  officers is a structural, not administrative, requirement.
- **Applications must be submitted within 60 business days of initiation** [A, defence.gov.au].
- **Processing: 2–3 months Entry Level; 4–6 months Levels 1–3** [A, consultancy sources citing
  Defence]. **Practitioners tell applicants to budget 6–12 months end-to-end** [A, multiple].
- **Cost to reach readiness: not found as an official figure.** [ESTIMATE: $10k–$30k for
  consultant support plus ML2 ICT uplift for a micro-business, plus $300–$600/user/yr ongoing
  licensing — reasoning: this is the band quoted by the DISP consultancies that surfaced in
  search, but I could not open their pricing pages to confirm. **Get three written quotes before
  you believe me.**]
- **Administration quality is poor and it is a known problem.** ANAO found Defence's DISP
  administration **"no more than partially effective"** with contracted-DISP arrangements only
  **"partially fit for purpose"**; at June 2021, **1,267 applications, 657 granted, 591 still
  awaiting processing** [A, ANAO Report 2021-22 No.4]. A **further ANAO audit of Defence's DISP
  contract administration is on the ANAO work program** [A, anao.gov.au]. Translation: the queue
  is real and the gate-keeper is under audit scrutiny, which usually makes gate-keepers *stricter*,
  not faster.

**Hermes verdict on DISP: it is a scalable barrier eventually, and the wrong first move now.**
$20k–50k and 6–12 months of a $50k budget, spent before a dollar of revenue, on a credential that
by itself wins nothing. DISP is what you buy **with** defence revenue, not **to get** it.

### 1.3 Security clearances — the chicken-and-egg, priced

- **An individual cannot sponsor their own clearance.** The sponsor must be a government entity or
  a DISP member organisation [A, AGSVA / WorkSec / Kinexus].
- **DISP membership lets you sponsor your own clearances — but not Positive Vetting** [A, Defence
  FAQ]. So: no DISP, no ability to sponsor; and DISP itself takes 6–12 months.
- **AGSVA fees (2025): Baseline $892 · NV1 $1,897 · NV2 $3,790**, plus sponsor pass-through
  employment-suitability and annual sponsorship fees [A, AusClear fee guide, 2025].
- **Timeframes: NV1 nominally ~70 business days, realistically 3–4 months, and 6–12 months for
  higher levels** [A, AusClear/WorkSec, 2026].

**The trap, stated plainly:** you need cleared staff to win cleared work, and you need a
contracted need-to-know to justify clearances. **The escape is not to break the loop — it is to
avoid it.** Two legitimate ways round:
1. **Supply labour into roles where the prime or Commonwealth sponsors the clearance.** You
   invoice; they carry the vetting.
2. **Sell only unclassified scopes** — garrison support, grounds and land management, training
   area maintenance, catering, cleaning, waste, logistics handling, warehousing, transport, event
   and training administration, workforce supply to non-cleared roles. **This is where the
   base-services money is.**

### 1.5 The other defence doors, ranked

1. **Exemption 16 of the Commonwealth Procurement Rules — the cheapest revenue in Australia.**
   Commonwealth buyers may **purchase directly from an Indigenous SME for contracts of any size
   and value**, with value-for-money established through **a simple quote process** [A, NIAA IPP
   Guidelines]. No tender. No panel. A phone call and a quote. **This is the single highest
   ratio of revenue-to-credential available to you**, and it costs nothing but sales effort.
   Defence is the biggest user of the IPP by a factor of two [A].
2. **Mandatory Minimum Requirements on high-value contracts.** AIC Plans are sought for Defence
   procurements **above $20m** and must evidence positive engagement with Australian SMEs and
   Indigenous business enterprises; **failure to meet AIC obligations can trigger contractual
   remedies and reputational risk, particularly for foreign primes seeking follow-on work** [A,
   Defence AIC Industry Guide / Jackson McDonald]. **This is a real lever**: the prime has a
   contractual and commercial reason to want you, which is a very different sales conversation
   from charity.
3. **AUKUS / submarine supply chain — not for you, and here is why.** DIVQ (est. 2024) and AUSSQ
   qualification programs; **60+ businesses progressing**; an additional **$21m** to expand AUSSQ;
   **H&B Defence engaging 18 more businesses** in composites, glass-reinforced plastics, furniture
   and joinery [A, ASA / minister.defence.gov.au 10 Apr 2026 / APDR]. **These are manufacturing
   qualification programs for firms with plant, ISO-grade quality systems and engineering
   staff.** You have no plant. Set this aside for 3+ years.
4. **ICT / cyber labour hire — currently closed.** **As at May 2026 the Digital Marketplace Panel 2
   (ICT labour hire) was not open for new seller applications**; entry is via AusTender when a
   refresh opens, with a Statement of Tax Record as minimum content [A, BuyICT/AusTender]. Register
   for notifications, but you cannot plan revenue against a closed panel.

### 1.6 Who eats you — defence

- **Established Indigenous defence primes.** The 18 firms holding 50% of IPP value [A]. They have
  DISP, cleared staff, past performance and Canberra relationships. **Reaction time to you
  entering: they will not notice, which is the point — you must find scopes below their floor.**
- **Downer, Ventia, Service Stream, MSS Security themselves.** They self-perform the profitable
  core and subcontract the peaks and the geographically awkward. **Their weakness: they must

- **Labour-hire aggregators** (Kinexus and similar cleared-workforce specialists [A, referenced in

  carrying regulatory risk it cannot absorb.**

  payroll business: you pay workers weekly and get paid on claim. **With under $50k you can fund

- **ACCHOs / NACCHO: the most *accessible* customer in health, and the smallest cheques.**
  ~145–150 Aboriginal Community Controlled Health Organisations nationally, NACCHO as peak with
  state/territory affiliates, funded substantially through the Indigenous Australians' Health
  Programme. **They buy:** locum and agency clinical staff, practice management and clinical ICT,
  medical consumables, patient transport, facilities services, training, program evaluation.
  **How they buy:** relationship-led, board-influenced, grant-cycle-timed, largely without formal
  panels. **Verify:** whether NACCHO or the state affiliates operate group purchasing
  arrangements you could join.

**Hermes verdict on health/care: no-go as a first move, on structure not on evidence.** Even if

is a genuine unmet-demand hypothesis worth testing later — but demand you cannot fund payroll to

## PART 3 — BANKING AND FINANCIAL SERVICES. **[TIER B — with one Tier A anchor]**

**Also curtailed by the search budget. The CPS 230 question you asked is the one part I did
research, and the answer is the useful bit.**

### 3.1 CPS 230 — does it make you harder to onboard? **Yes for material work, no for the work you'd actually get.**

- **CPS 230 applied from 1 July 2025** [A, APRA / MinterEllison / Gatekeeper].
- **The stringent obligations attach to "material service providers"** — those relied on for a
  critical operation or exposing the entity to material operational risk. **Contracts with MSPs
  must contain seven mandatory clauses**: service levels, risk management, security, **audit and
  regulator access**, subcontracting, contingency, and data ownership [A].
- **Non-significant financial institutions received a 12-month extension** on the business
  continuity and scenario-analysis requirements, in response to proportionality concerns from
  smaller entities [A, APRA response to submissions].

**The decisive finding you asked for, stated precisely:** CPS 230 has **materially raised the bar
for becoming a *material* service provider** to a bank or insurer — a micro-business cannot
credibly sign a contract granting APRA audit access, guaranteeing continuity and carrying data
ownership obligations. **But CPS 230 does not touch non-material suppliers**, and that is where
supplier-diversity spend actually sits: facilities, cleaning, catering, print, uniforms, events,
merchandise, creative and design, cultural awareness training, recruitment support. **So the
honest answer is: CPS 230 did not close the door you were going to use. It permanently closed the
door to the high-value door.** Your ceiling in financial services is now structurally low.

### 3.2 What actually blocks you at a bank [B — verify]

Not CPS 230. The vendor-master onboarding gate:
- **Financial viability assessment** — typically 2–3 years of financial statements. **A barely
  trading entity fails here first.** This is the real exclusion.
- **Insurance minimums** — public liability commonly $10m–$20m, professional indemnity $5m–$10m,
  workers compensation, and increasingly cyber liability. [B — verify actual thresholds with each
  bank's supplier portal.]
- **Information security questionnaire**, modern slavery statement, code of conduct attestation.
- **Published Indigenous supplier spend by the major banks: not found.** All four majors run
  Reconciliation Action Plans with Indigenous procurement commitments, but **I did not retrieve a
  single verified spend figure and I am not going to invent one.** [B — pull each bank's latest
  RAP and annual/sustainability report; the number is usually disclosed.]

**Hermes verdict on financial services: no-go as a revenue strategy, yes as a logo strategy —
later.** Bank supplier-diversity spend is small relative to their total supplier base, concentrated
in low-value categories, slow to onboard (**3–9 months is typical for vendor master setup** [B]),
and structurally capped for you by CPS 230. **The one thing it is genuinely good for is
reference-ability**: a big-four logo on a capability statement helps in every other sector. Pursue
it opportunistically at zero cost, never as the plan.

customer's badge**, and the past performance you accumulate is defence past performance.

### The 24-month sequence

- **Months 0–1 — eligibility.** Fix 51% ownership/control [A: required since 1 Jul 2026]. Move
  Registration → Certification. Insurances. Register on the three prime subcontractor portals and
  AusTender. **Total: under $6,000.**
- **Months 1–6 — first revenue.** Sell unclassified scopes to Downer/Ventia/Service Stream in your
  home region and directly to Commonwealth buyers under Exemption 16. **Target: $80k–$250k
  contracted.** Hire only against signed work.
- **Months 6–12 — past performance and references.** Deliver flawlessly; collect written
  performance references — **these, not credentials, are what you are actually short of.** Add a
  prequal system only when a prime names one. Opportunistically open a bank supplier-diversity
  conversation for the logo.
- **Months 12–24 — buy the next rung with revenue, not capital.** ISO 9001 if a named tender
  requires it. Essential Eight ML2, then **DISP Entry Level**, funded from margin. Appoint CSO/SO
  from the staff you now have. **Only now** is DISP a rational purchase.
- **Months 24+ — the large outcome.** Cleared workforce supply, sustainment and logistics scopes,
  and prime AIC-driven partnerships where **your Indigenous participation is contractually
  valuable to their bid** [A]. That is the path to scale, and it does not exist without months 0–24.

## KILL SHOTS — ranked by probability

1. **You are already IPP-ineligible (50% not 51%).** [A, threshold effective 1 Jul 2026] —
   **highest probability, highest impact, cheapest to check.** If true, everything in this memo is
   void until the cap table is fixed. **Check this week.**
2. **The set-aside is already consolidated and you never reach the tail.** 18 firms hold 50% of
   $7bn; the winners are Canberra-based and hold ten years of past performance [A, Feb 2026]. You
   may find that every scope you can do is already held by an incumbent Indigenous supplier. **Test
   by counting quote requests, not meetings.**
3. **Credential spend eats the capital before revenue arrives.** DISP + ML2 + clearances is
   $20k–$50k and 6–12 months with no attached revenue [A/EST]. **This is the standard way
   capital-poor businesses die in defence, and optimistic advisers cause it.** Mitigated only by
   refusing to buy rows 8–13 of the ladder in year one.
4. **Working capital, not credentials, is the binding constraint.** Primes pay 30–60 days [B];
   payroll is weekly. **Under $50k funds roughly 2–4 subcontractors through one cycle** [ESTIMATE].
   Growth in a labour business consumes cash. **You can win work and still fail.**
5. **The 3% Indigenous participation clause may not sit in the Base Services contracts.** I found
   the 3% figure in general AIC contract language, **not** in those three contracts [A/not found].
   If the primes have no contractual Indigenous target, your leverage drops from "they need you" to
   "they might like you", and the pipeline estimate in §1.4 is wrong.
6. **Tier B risk.** Health/care and financial services in this memo are **recall, not research**.
   If the plan ever pivots toward them, **the research must be re-run first.**

   since 1 Jul 2026]. Call Supply Nation, get the Certification requirements and fee **in writing**
   (**this memo could not find the fee**). Bind PL/PI insurance. Register on Ventia's public
   subcontractor portal and the Downer and Service Stream equivalents [A].
2. **Weeks 1–4 — 25 qualified conversations (cost: phone and fuel).**
   - **3–6 calls:** Indigenous participation / SME supply-chain leads at **Downer, Ventia, Service
     Stream, MSS Security**. Ask one question: *"What scopes in my region are you still sourcing,
     and what is your Indigenous participation target under this contract?"* **That question also
     resolves kill shot #5.**
   - **5 calls:** Defence regional estate and garrison managers in your state.
   - **1 email:** `indigenous.procurement@defence.gov.au` [A, address surfaced in Defence material].
   - **10 calls:** Commonwealth agency procurement contacts, pitching **Exemption 16** explicitly
     by name — *"you can buy from me directly with a simple quote"* [A].
   - **3 calls:** local ACCHOs, to price-test the Tier B health hypothesis at zero cost.
3. **Attend one Supply Nation or Defence industry engagement event** in your capital city.

**What each result means:**
- **≥3 written scope invitations or quote requests → GO.** The Indigenous lever converts.
  Proceed to the months 1–6 plan.
- **1–2 → GO WITH CHANGES.** Narrow to the one region and one service line that responded and
  re-run for 30 days.

  responsiveness, and revisit defence when you have staff and references.

**Note what is NOT in this test: no DISP, no clearances, no ISO, no NDIS registration.** If the
demand is real, those get bought later out of revenue. If it isn't, you still have your $50k.
