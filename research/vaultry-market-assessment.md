# Vaultry — market assessment

**Question:** is there a legitimate gap in helping ANZ civil subcontractors win work and
manage documents/compliance, and can it produce a large outcome?

**Verdict: No to the venture as framed. Yes to a smaller, real business — and there is one
genuinely interesting adjacent opportunity that the research surfaced by accident.**

Five Hermes agents worked independent fronts — incumbents, demand channels, market size,
business model, regulation. They were not shown each other's work. All five independently
reached the same structural conclusion:

> **The party with the pain has no budget. The party with the budget wants the pain to continue.**

That is not a gap waiting to be filled. That is an equilibrium, and it is load-bearing for
the incumbents.

---

## 1. The two halves of the idea, judged separately

### "Help subcontractors get work" — no-go, and this is measured, not argued

Felix (ASX:FLX) ran this exact experiment for 14 years with distribution no startup can buy:
CPB Contractors and Downer *mandate* that new subcontractors register through it.

| Side of market | ARR (30 Jun 2026) | Per customer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vendor marketplace (subcontractors) | $1.8m | ~$20/vendor/yr, **flat YoY** |
| Enterprise (head contractors) | $7.5m | ~$99,000/customer/yr, growing |

Felix launched as PlantMiner in 2012-13 selling exactly "help civil subs get work", then
pivoted away from vendor monetisation because the builders were the ones who would pay.
Listed at $0.47 (Jan 2021); $0.06 (Jun 2026).

Corroborating: Oneflare — Domain paid $15m for 35%, Airtasker bought the assets for $9.8m,
retired entirely 30 Jun 2026. hipages, the best-run AU trades marketplace in a segment with
10-100x civil's job frequency, grew tradies 1% YoY at $2,497 ARPU — the proven ANZ ceiling
for demand generation. Levelset sold to Procore for US$500m with 250,000 users and 1.3%
paying, because it sold *statutory lien deadlines*, not leads.

**Why it fails structurally:** discovery is already free (GETS, EstimateOne's free tier,
Austroads registers, tier-1 portals). The subcontractor's binding constraint is a **gate,
not a list** — prequal class, referee reports, a vendor record. Selling a better list to
someone stopped by a gate is selling the wrong product.

### "Manage docs and compliance" — crowded from both ends

The pitch "one profile, many head contractors" is already incumbent marketing copy:

- **Cm3** sells "one fee, one year, unlimited clients" (A$399-3,045/yr by risk tier)
- **HammerTech** (US$70m from Riverwood, 2023/24) is **free to subcontractors**
- **ComplyFlow** is free to contractors and suppliers
- **Austroads National Prequalification System (Road & Bridge)** — "no fee to apply"
- **Tōtika** (CHASNZ) already solves duplication in NZ, industry-endorsed, JAS-ANZ audited

You cannot beat free that is funded by someone else's budget.

**Who pays, settled:** head contractors >> subcontractors under mandate > subcontractors
voluntarily. LinkSafe is the proof — it sells principals a *menu* of who to bill. The payer
is a setting in someone else's contract. No evidence was found anywhere of subcontractors
voluntarily paying meaningfully for compliance absent a mandate. They pay for leads
(EstimateOne), faster payment (Siteline), or capital (Billd).

## 2. The ceiling, arithmetically

Bottom-up, cross-checked two ways landing within 7% (size-band arithmetic vs CCF's 1,800 +
CCNZ's 800 members at ~50% penetration):

```
TAM = (11,750 x A$900) + (4,230 x A$3,000) + (610 x A$15,000) + (28 x A$60,000)
    = A$34.1m/yr at 100% share
SAM = A$21.8m/yr    (the ~4,868 firms with 5+ employees)
SOM = A$2.2-4.4m ARR over 5 years (487-974 customers)
```

| Target | Requires | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A$1m ARR | 67 customers @ A$1,250/mo — 11% of the 20-199 band | Achievable; cheapest path |
| A$10m ARR @ A$500/mo | 1,667 customers — 34% of the entire universe | Needs a *growing* market |
| A$10m ARR @ A$1,250/mo | 667 customers — **109% of the 20-199 band** | Impossible |

And the market is **contracting**: engineering construction work done -5.1% y/y (Jun qtr
2026); Road & Bridge forecast -18% over two years; transport pipeline falling from
$11.4bn/qtr (Q1 2024) toward $5.2bn/qtr by 2029; NSW infrastructure -$2.2bn in real terms.

**On "can this make me rich":** a venture-scale exit needs A$12-17m ARR, which this market
cannot produce at any credible price. What it *can* produce is ~A$2-4m ARR at 80% margins
with 3-6 people — roughly A$1.5-3m/yr to an owner, trading at maybe A$8-20m. Excellent if
you own all of it. **Poor if you raise institutional money against it**, because the fund's
case cannot be produced by this TAM. That distinction matters more than the number.

## 3. Three findings that change how you'd build

**The pain is already a salary line, not a software line.** Over half of small builders
spend 5+ hours/week on regulatory tasks and 68% have considered scaling back over red tape —
but **56% have already hired or redeployed staff to absorb it**. There is no unspent
compliance budget. You would be displacing a A$45-70k admin: a 3-6 month consultative sale,
not a self-serve signup. A 20-person sub already spends A$20-30k/yr on software, so asking
A$3-12k makes you a *replacement* — and you must name what you replace.

**AI deflates this product rather than enabling it.** Framework AI ships "Form Fill" — AI
prequal/tender form completion — in September 2026. If filling the form is free, the fee to
store the form does not survive. HammerTech is spending its US$70m in the same direction.

**The category dies by cheap absorption, not announcement**, so the failure rate looks lower
than it is: Damstra A$2.44 -> A$0.24 and delisted; Felix -63% since IPO; Assignar moved HQ
from Sydney to Denver; Sitemate took ~9 years to a Series A. Every real exit in the space —
Payapps ~US$390m, Levelset US$500m, BuildingConnected US$275m, Flashtract to Trimble —
attaches to **payments or bid flow**, enterprise-paid. None is a document library.

## 4. What actually survived

Three things, in ascending order of interest.

**a. Invert the payer.** Sell supply-chain assurance to the principal; make the
subcontractor's seat free. This is the only configuration with a demonstrated revenue base
(~$99k/customer at Felix vs ~$20/vendor). It is also the crowded configuration.

**b. Currency, not forms.** The law is forking, not converging: Victoria sits outside WHS
harmonisation with its own SOP model commenced 15 Apr 2026 (retrospective to existing
contracts); WA retention trusts from A$20,000 since 1 Feb 2024; Qld frozen; NZ moving to a
critical-risk model 1 Apr 2027. Critically, **NSW re-made its entire WHS Regulation (SL 2025
No 440), renumbering clauses to sections — so every SWMS and WHS plan in NSW citing
"clause 291" is now stale.** That is a re-papering event with a date. Forms are a commodity;
knowing which version is current across eight jurisdictions is not.

**c. The payment-documents wedge — the strongest positive signal in the body of work.**
Three agents reached it independently from different directions:

- Market sizing named "take a cut of flow — payments, retentions, insurance" as the *only*
  route where A$10m ARR comes from ~500 customers rather than ~4,000.
- Business model found every real exit attaches to payments or bid flow.
- Regulation found Queensland **explicitly named "lack of adequate accounting software to
  support trustees to comply"** as a reason for pausing project trust account rollout —
  a government putting the software gap in writing.

Payment documentation is the one place where the document is not discretionary paperwork
but the thing that releases cash: NSW SOPA s13(7)-(9) supporting statements (an offence to
omit; a separate offence, with imprisonment available, for a knowingly false one); NZ
statutory retention reporting to the subcontractor at least quarterly since 5 Oct 2023 with
director penalties to $50k; WA retention trusts.

**The caution:** Qld's trust rollout is paused with no new date, Payapps is already inside
Autodesk, and this is adjacent to — not the same as — what Vaultry is prototyping today.
It is a lead to validate, not a conclusion.

## 5. Kill shots, ranked

1. **A$22m serviceable market, contracting for three years** (~85%)
2. **Compliance software price-anchored at zero for subs** — HammerTech free, ComplyFlow
   free, Austroads free (~70%)
3. **Pain lives in a salary line, not a software line** (~65%)
4. **No write-path.** The moat is not storage, it is pushing verified documents *into* Cm3,
   Avetta, Rapid and LinkSafe. None publishes a partner API; all four are now PE-owned with
   every incentive to keep subcontractor data captive. Without it you are selling browser
   automation against four hostile portals (~65%)
5. **Customer mortality below 5 employees.** Construction is 26-27% of all AU corporate
   failures; >75% of failing firms had <19 FTEs (~60%)

## 6. The 30-day test — build nothing

Every memo independently recommended primary interviews over further desk research, because
**the decisive number does not exist publicly.** No survey, association submission, or
inquiry was found quantifying how many portals a typical civil sub pays for. The 3-5
estimate underpinning the entire "compliance tax" thesis is inference, not measurement.

1. **Pull the ABS CABEE cube** (Division E x subdivision x employment size) and the ATO
   Small Business Benchmarks. Half a day, A$0. **Stop if the real 5+ employee count is
   below 4,000.**
2. **Twenty calls into the 20-199 band** (CCF and CCNZ rosters are a public named universe).
   One question does most of the work: *"Name an invoice you personally paid to get on site —
   the amount, and who sent it."* **Stop if fewer than 5 of 10 can name one** — the budget
   is upstream.
3. **Price test before building:** a one-page offer at A$1,250/mo to 30 firms, asking for a
   A$500 deposit. **Stop if fewer than 3 of 30 deposit**, or if more than half say
   "HammerTech already does that free."
4. **Mandate test:** five tier-1 procurement leads — would you accept a third-party evidence
   feed at all? **Stop if 4+ are locked to Felix/Ariba.** Downer has renewed to ~FY29.

**Green light:** 5+ deposits *and* three or more firms independently describing the
alternative as "hiring another admin."

## 7. Confidence and what must be re-checked

All primary sources were egress-blocked this session (ABS, Stats NZ, ASIC, IBISWorld,
legislation registries, vendor pricing pages). Search worked; fetching did not. Figures are
search-synthesised, not read off the page. See `METHODOLOGY.md`.

Three specific items must be verified before anyone repeats them:

- **Do not repeat:** a search summary claimed the SWMS fall-height threshold rose from 2m to
  3m in July 2026. It could not be corroborated from any source.
- The commencement date of the NSW WHS Regulation 2025 — sources conflict between 22 Aug
  2025, a 1 Sep 2025 repeal deadline, and Jan 2026 commentary.
- Whether NSW and Victoria genuinely have no general licensing regime for civil contractors.
  If true, it materially shrinks the "licensing forces documentation" argument in the two
  largest civil markets.

One credibility warning: the NSW Building Commission expansion (Fair Trading and Building
Legislation Amendment Bill 2026, passed 5 Aug 2026) touches only residential and vertical
construction. **It is close to irrelevant to civil.** Citing it as a tailwind in an investor
conversation would be a credibility risk.
