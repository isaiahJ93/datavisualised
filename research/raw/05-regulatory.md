## Verdict

**Go-with-changes — but change *who you sell to*, not what you build.**

The regulatory stack in AU/NZ civil construction absolutely creates recurring, dated,
hand-over-to-a-third-party documents. There are at least five of them and they fire monthly or
quarterly, not annually. That part of the thesis survives.

What does not survive contact with the evidence is the **budget** assumption. Read the mandates
by *who they bind*: the money-side duties — WHS management plan (reg 309), obtaining SWMS before
high risk work starts (reg 312), supporting statements under NSW SOPA s13(7), retention/project
trust accounting, industrial manslaughter exposure at $18–20m — **bind the principal contractor,
not the subcontractor**. The subcontractor's obligations (produce a SWMS, hold a white card, keep
a plant maintenance record) are real and dated, but the subbie's *incentive to pay for software*
is derived: it exists because a head contractor withholds site access or withholds payment.
That is a channel fact, not a pain fact, and it means the head contractor's prequalification
platform is structurally between you and your customer.

The single strongest piece of evidence in this memo for an unmet software budget is not a safety
regulation at all: on **31 January 2025 the Queensland Government paused phases 3 and 4 of the
project trust account rollout and named "lack of adequate accounting software to support trustees
to comply with their obligations under the framework" as one of the stated reasons.** A government
put in writing that the software does not exist. That is worth more than any amount of survey pain.

And the one clean future commencement date to time a launch to — **1 April 2027, NZ Health and
Safety at Work Amendment Act 2026** — is **deregulatory**. It narrows small-PCBU duties to
critical risks and creates an approved-code-of-practice safe harbour. If you launch into it
selling "more paperwork", you launch into a headwind. Sell "prove you are inside the narrower
duty" instead.

non-trivial second build, not a config flag.

### 1.2 NSW re-made its entire WHS Regulation in 2025 — every clause number moved

The **Work Health and Safety Regulation 2025 (NSW), SL 2025 No 440** repeals and replaces the WHS
Regulation 2017, which was otherwise due for automatic repeal on **1 September 2025** under
s10(2) of the Subordinate Legislation Act 1989. **[V]**

- Provisions are now **"sections", not "clauses"**, and drafting has been modernised to NSW PCO
  standards **[V1]**.
- Adds hierarchy-of-control application to **psychosocial risks**, and new information-sharing
  duties for the **silica worker register** **[V1]**.
- Gives SafeWork NSW power to authorise training providers and courses and charge fees **[V1]**.

**Commencement date — CONFLICTED, resolve before citing.** Secondary commentary says
**22 August 2025**; the legislation.nsw.gov.au point-in-time URL is dated 3 October 2025; and a
Clyde & Co piece titled "The Commencement of the Work Health and Safety Regulation 2025 (NSW)"
is dated **January 2026**, implying at least some provisions commenced later. I could not open

### 1.3 SWMS for High Risk Construction Work — the core recurring artefact

**Regulation 291 — Meaning of high risk construction work.** Defines the **18 categories** of HRCW
that trigger a mandatory SWMS. Adopted in NSW, Qld, WA, SA, Tas, ACT, NT. **[V]**
Sources: [NT WorkSafe — SWMS for HRCW](https://worksafe.nt.gov.au/forms-and-resources/bulletins/safe-work-method-statement-for-high-risk-construction-work);
[AustLII Qld WHS Reg 2011 reg 291](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/qld/consol_reg/whasr2011309/s291.html);
[WorkSafe WA — SWMS for HRCW](https://www.worksafe.wa.gov.au/publications/safe-work-method-statements-high-risk-construction-work);
[WorkSafe ACT SWMS Guidance Note 2025](https://www.worksafe.act.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/2192124/SWMS-Guidance-Note-2025-2.pdf).

> **⚠ Unverified claim, flagged deliberately.** One search summary asserted that "WHS Regulation 291
> was amended in July 2026 increasing the fall height threshold from 2 metres to 3 metres for
> residential construction." I could **not** corroborate this from any source and it conflicts with
> the model regulation as I know it. **Do not repeat this.** It needs a primary-source check. If it
> *is* true it is a launch-timing event; if it is a search hallucination it is exactly the kind of
> thing that gets a founder laughed out of a customer meeting.

**What a SWMS must contain** (model reg 299 and following): identify the high risk construction
work; state the hazards and risks to health and safety; describe the control measures; describe
how the controls will be **implemented, monitored and reviewed**; be prepared having regard to
conditions at the workplace **and the WHS management plan**; and be set out so the people using it
can read and follow it. **[V]**

**The duty chain (model numbering; NSW numbering has moved — see §1.2):**

| Reg | Duty | Binds | Recurring doc? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 291 | Defines the 18 HRCW categories | — | — |
| **299** | **SWMS must be prepared before HRCW starts** | The **PCBU carrying out the HRCW = the subcontractor** | **Yes — per activity, per site** |
| 300 | Work must be carried out in accordance with the SWMS; if not, stop and fix | Subcontractor | Yes — evidence of compliance |
| 301–303 | SWMS given to principal contractor; reviewed and revised; **copy kept for 2 years after a notifiable incident** | Subcontractor | **Yes — handover + retention** |

Exact numbering of 300–303 varies slightly between jurisdictions and my two sources disagreed on
which of 301/302/303 is "review" vs "copy to principal contractor" vs "keep". **[V1]** — confirm
per jurisdiction before hard-coding reg references into product copy.

**Penalties (model, as reported by secondary sources — [V1], secondary only):**
reg 299 failure to prepare a SWMS — max **$6,000 individual / $30,000 body corporate**;
reg 300 failure to carry out work in accordance with the SWMS — same;
on-the-spot fines reported at **$144 / $720** for failing to keep a copy 2 years and
**$432 / $2,160** for failing to review and revise.
Sources: [SiteBook — fines for non-compliance with WHS](https://web.sitebook.com.au/features/fines-for-non-compliance-with-whs/);
[SafetyCheck — fines for safe work breaches](https://safetycheck.co/construction-safety/fines-for-safe-work-breaches/);
[AustLII Qld reg 299](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/qld/consol_reg/whasr2011309/s299.html).

> **Adversarial note:** $6,000 is not a budget-creating penalty. **The SWMS fine is not why anyone
> buys software.** The reason is reg 312 (below) — the head contractor cannot let you start until
> it holds your SWMS, so a missing SWMS is a *stand-down*, and a stand-down is priced in day rates,
> not penalty units. Sell the stand-down, not the fine.

### 1.4 Principal contractor duties — regs 309–315: the transmission mechanism

This is the single most important block in the memo, because **it is the regulation that makes the
head contractor demand documents from the subcontractor.**

| Reg | Duty | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| **309** | **Written WHS management plan must be prepared before work starts** | Construction project — construction work where the **contract price is $250,000 or more** **[V]** |
| **310** | Principal contractor must ensure, SFAIRP, each person about to carry out construction work is made aware of the plan and their right to inspect it **before commencing work** | Every worker, every subbie **[V]** |
| **311** | Keep the plan **up to date** and make people aware of revisions **[V1]** | Continuous |
| **312** | **Where the project involves HRCW, the principal contractor must obtain the SWMS before that work starts** **[V1]** | Every HRCW activity by every sub |
| **313** | **Keep a copy of the WHS management plan for the duration of the project** **[V1]** | Whole project |
| **314** | Compliance duties across Part 3.2 Divisions 2–5 and 7–10 and Part 4.4 **[V1]** | — |
| **315** | Further specific duties — traffic management, storage of plant, disposal of construction material, essential services **[V1]** | Site-wide |

Sources: [AustLII Qld WHS Reg reg 309](https://www5.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/qld/consol_reg/whasr2011309/s309.html);
[AustLII NT WHS (NUL) Reg reg 310](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/nt/consol_reg/whasulr2011606/s310.html);
[AustLII Cth WHS Reg reg 309](https://www5.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_reg/whasr2011327/s309.html);
[Comcare — contractor management guidance for PCBUs](https://www.comcare.gov.au/sites/default/files/docs/contractor-management-guidance-for-pcbus.pdf).

**The $250,000 threshold is the number to build the TAM on.** Essentially every civil job above a
driveway is a "construction project", which means essentially every civil subcontractor is working
under someone who has a reg 309 plan and a reg 312 obligation to hold their SWMS before they start.

### 1.5 The consult / cooperate / coordinate duty — why documents flow *down*

Model WHS Act **ss 46–48**: where two or more PCBUs have duties in relation to the same matter,
each must **consult, cooperate and coordinate** with the others so far as is reasonably practicable.
**[U — not verified this session; section numbers from prior knowledge.]** This is the legal hook
head contractors cite when they demand a subcontractor's SWMS, licences, plant records, inductions,
insurances and prequal — and it is why the demand is *documentary*: the only way to evidence
"consultation" after an incident is paper.

Combined with **reg 39** (information, training and instruction) and the **primary duty s19**,
this is what turns a general duty into a document register. **[U]**

### 1.6 Construction induction ("white card") — regs 316–318

A PCBU **must not direct or allow a worker to carry out construction work** unless the worker has
successfully completed general construction induction training (currently **CPCWHS1001 — Prepare to
work safely in the construction industry**), and **if the training was completed more than 2 years
previously, the worker must have carried out construction work in the preceding 2 years.** **[V]**

The PCBU must ensure the worker **holds the card**, or — if they have applied but not yet been
issued one — **holds a statement of attainment issued within the preceding 60 days.** **[V]**

Sources: [WHS Management Systems — reg s316–s318 white card requirements](https://whsmanagementsystems.au/regulation-s316-s318-construction-induction-white-card);
[ASQA — construction induction (white) card](https://www.asqa.gov.au/students/student-record/construction-induction-white-card);
[WorkSafe ACT — white cards](https://www.worksafe.act.gov.au/licensing-and-registration/white-cards-construction-induction).

**Recurring document test: YES, and it is the sneaky one.** The card itself does not expire — but
the *entitlement to rely on it* lapses if the holder has not done construction work in the last
two years. That is a rolling, per-worker, evidence-of-recency obligation that sits on the employer
and cannot be discharged by a photocopy in a filing cabinet. It is a natural database product.

### 1.7 High risk work licences, plant registration and VOCs

- **HRW licences** are issued by state/territory regulators and are **nationally recognised under
  mutual recognition** — a licence issued in one jurisdiction is valid in all. **[V1]**
- **Plant registration:** **Schedule 5** of the WHS Regulations lists plant *designs* and *items of
  plant* requiring registration. **Item registration is valid for 5 years** and the registration
  number must be displayed on the plant where it is easy to see. **[V1]**
- **Records of inspection and maintenance must be kept for the life of the plant while it is under
  your control**; for high-risk plant, registration documents, engineering assessments and
  certification records must also be retained. **[V1]**

Sources: [WorkSafe Qld — what plant requires registration](https://www.worksafe.qld.gov.au/licensing-and-registrations/plant-registration/what-type-of-plant-requires-registration);
[Comcare — plant registration](https://www.comcare.gov.au/safe-healthy-work/licences-registrations-authorisations/plant);
[Safe Work Australia — plant supply, design and registration](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/safety-topic/managing-health-and-safety/plant-supply-design-and-registration/overview);
[Model Code of Practice — Managing the risks of plant in the workplace, Nov 2024](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-11/model_code_of_practice-managing_the_risks_of_plant_in_the_workplace-nov24.pdf);
[WA — registration of an item of plant](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/multi-step-guides/plant-and-design-registration/registration-of-item-of-plant-application).

- **Verification of Competency (VOC): there is no statute that mandates a VOC.** VOC is a
  *contractual and prequalification* artefact that head contractors and asset owners impose,
  derived from the s19 primary duty and the reg 39 training duty. **[U — inference, labelled as
  such.]** Do not describe VOCs as a legal requirement in marketing; a HSEQ manager will catch it.
  Describe them accurately as a client-imposed condition of site access, which is *more* compelling
  because it has a named person withholding a gate pass.

## 3. Security of Payment — documentation that is directly tied to cash

This is where a missing document costs a subcontractor money *this month*, not risk *someday*.
On a pure willingness-to-pay basis this outranks safety.

### 3.1 NSW — Building and Construction Industry Security of Payment Act 1999

- **s13** payment claim must: be in writing; identify the construction contract; identify claimant
  and recipient; contain sufficient detail to identify the work/goods/services. **[V]**
- **s13(7)–(9) — the supporting statement.** A head contractor serving a payment claim on a
  principal **must accompany it with a supporting statement in the form approved by the Secretary**,
  declaring that **all subcontractors have been paid all amounts due and payable**. **[V]**
  - **s13(7)** — offence to serve a claim without the supporting statement. **[V]**
  - **s13(8)** — separate offence to serve a claim with a supporting statement **known to be false
    or misleading in a material particular**; imprisonment is available for individuals. **[V]**
  - Exact penalty units and the 3-month imprisonment figure: **[U]**, not confirmed this session.

Sources: [AustLII NSW SOPA s13](https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/nsw/consol_act/bacisopa1999606/s13.html);
[EstimateOne — SOPA NSW subbie guide](https://estimateone.com/for-subbies/security-of-payment-act-nsw/);
[HFK Lawyers](https://www.hfklawyers.com.au/article/getting-payment-right-security-of-payment-act-nsw/);
[Merlo Law — definitive guide to SOP and adjudication in NSW](https://www.merlolaw.com.au/post/the-definitive-guide-to-security-of-payment-laws-and-adjudication-in-new-south-wales);
[Contracts Specialist — valid payment claim requirements](https://www.contractsspecialist.com.au/valid-payment-claim-requirements-nsw/).

**Recurring document test: YES — monthly, per project, signed, handed upstream, with criminal
exposure for a false one.** This is the highest-frequency, highest-consequence recurring document
in the entire memo. Note *who* it binds: the **head contractor**. The subcontractor's role is to
supply the evidence that makes the head contractor's declaration true — which is precisely why
head contractors run subcontractor payment-declaration portals.

### 3.2 Victoria — reforms commenced 15 April 2026 [MAJOR, and already live]

The **Building Legislation Amendment (Fairer Payments on Jobsites and Other Matters) Act 2025 (Vic)**
amended the Building and Construction Industry Security of Payment Act 2002 (Vic), **commencing
15 April 2026.** **[V]**

- **"Excluded amounts" and "reference dates" abolished** — a progress payment can now generally be

- **A 20-business-day statutory cap on payment terms** — a first for Victoria. **[V]**
- **New notice of intention to have recourse to performance security: at least 5 business days
  before recourse**, identifying the contract, the provisions relied on, the amount, and the
  circumstances entitling recourse. **[V]**
- **Applies retrospectively to existing contracts and to performance security already held**
  under them. **[V]**

### 3.5 New Zealand — Construction Contracts Act 2002 + retentions regime

The **Construction Contracts (Retention Money) Amendment Act 2023 (No 12)** came into force
**5 October 2023**, applying to commercial construction contracts entered into or renewed on or
after that date. **[V]**

- Retention money is **trust property**, held on trust by the payer for the payee. **[V]**
- **Reporting: the holder must give the party entitled to payment information about the retention
  money when it is first held, and then AT LEAST ONCE EVERY THREE MONTHS** — including details of
  the account or instrument holding the money. **[V]**
- **Offences include:** failure to comply with accounting, recording and reporting requirements;
  using retention money for a purpose other than remedying defects; failure to provide regular
  information to the subcontractor; intentionally providing false information; failing to comply
  with MBIE information requests or obstructing MBIE. **[V]**
- **Penalties (secondary sources, figures inconsistent between them — [V1]):** up to **$50,000 for a
  director and $200,000 for a company** for failing to hold retention money properly; **up to
  $50,000 per offence** for reporting failures; **$20,000 (company) / $50,000 (each director)** for
  misuse of retention money.

## 4. Labour / IR — records demanded from subcontractors

### 4.1 Same Job Same Pay (Closing Loopholes) — regulated labour hire arrangement orders

- Applications to the Fair Work Commission for a **regulated labour hire arrangement order** have
  been available since **late 2023**; **the first orders took effect in November 2024.** **[V]**
- Effect: labour hire employees must be paid **at least the full rate of pay** of a comparable
  employee under the **host employer's enterprise agreement** doing the same job. **[V]**
- **Carve-outs:** the FWC must not make an order where the work is **wholly or mainly the provision
  of a service rather than the supply of labour**, or where it is not fair and reasonable; and the
  protected rate generally does not apply to **engagements of 3 months or less.** **[V]**
- **s306H FWA — the documentation duty:** a **regulated host** covered by an order must, **on
  written request from the labour hire employer**, provide information about the **protected rate of
  pay** for the employer's regulated employees, as soon as reasonably practicable and in time for
  the employer to comply. **[V]**
- ~**7,600 workers** across mining, aviation, rail, meat processing, automotive, water treatment,
  **construction**, public administration and warehousing had benefited as at the government's
  count. **[V1]** (Date of count not stated in source.)

### 4.2 Wage theft criminalised — 1 January 2025

Intentional underpayment became a **criminal offence** under the Fair Work Act on **1 January 2025**,
carrying **up to 10 years imprisonment** for individuals. Captured payments include wages, super,
redundancy pay, leave, overtime, penalty rates, allowances and leave loading. Intent must be proven —
honest errors attract civil penalties, not criminal liability. **[V]**

### 4.3 Sham contracting and the independent contractor definition — 26 August 2024

- The defence to a sham contracting claim changed from a **"recklessness"** test to a
  **"reasonableness"** test, effective **26 August 2024**. The employer must now prove it
  **reasonably believed** the worker was a contractor, judged against the size and nature of the
  enterprise, the employer's skills and experience, the industry, whether there is in-house HR
  expertise, and whether professional advice was sought and followed. **[V]**
- Penalties for sham contracting were increased. **[V1]**
- Independent contractor changes (new statutory approach to characterisation) also commenced
  **26 August 2024**. **[V1]**

## 5. Tax and payment reporting

### 5.1 TPAR — Taxable Payments Annual Report (building and construction)

- **Due 28 August each year** for the preceding financial year. The **2025–26** TPAR is due
  **28 August 2026**. **[V]**
- **Who must lodge:** businesses where payments to contractors for services are a significant share
  of income — reported as **50% or more for building and construction** (10% or more for most other
  covered services). **[V1 — the exact ATO test has several limbs; confirm before publishing.]**
- **What must be reported per contractor:** ABN, name, address, **gross amount paid including GST**,
  and the **GST component**. **[V]** Payments must be reported even where materials are included;
  if a payment covers both labour and materials, **the full amount is reported.** **[V]**
- **Penalty:** failure-to-lodge penalty framework as for an overdue BAS — reported at **$313 per
  28-day period for small entities, max $1,565**; larger entities proportionally higher.
  **[V1 — the Commonwealth penalty unit is indexed; this figure's as-of date is unclear.]**

Sources: [Xero — TPAR compliance guide](https://www.xero.com/au/guides/tpar-compliance/);

### 7.3 New Zealand Health and Safety at Work Amendment Act 2026 — **the launch date**

- **Royal Assent: 9 July 2026.** **[V]**
- **Most substantive changes take effect 1 April 2027** — **pushed back from the original
  1 November 2026.** **[V]**
- **Focus on "critical risks"** — risks that can result in death, serious injury or illness. The
  test is now **whether serious harm would result if the risk occurs, not how probable the risk is
  to occur.** **[V]**
- **"Small PCBU" = a business with fewer than 20 employees; their duties are NARROWED to critical
  risks** (while continuing to provide essential worker welfare facilities). **[V]**
- **From 1 April 2027, a PCBU that follows the relevant actions in an eligible approved code of
  practice is TREATED AS HAVING MET the corresponding legal duty — a statutory "safe harbour."**
  **[V]**
- Clarifies the **governance vs operational management** split to reduce director overcompliance.
  **[V]**
- WorkSafe's main objective realigned to critical risk; functions prioritised across guidance,
  ACOPs, Safe Work Instruments, monitoring and enforcement. **[V]**
- Seismic-risk exception where the building owner complies with the Building Act 2004. **[V]**

## 9. Kill shots — ranked by probability

1. **The mandates bind the principal, the cost lands on the sub, and the sub has no budget line.**
   Regs 309–315, NSW s13(7), industrial manslaughter and the OFSC scheme all attach to the head
   contractor. The sub's spend is a *condition of entry* imposed by someone else — which is exactly
   the structure that lets incumbent prequal schemes (Cm3, Avetta, Rapid Global, LinkSafe, Pegasus
   — **names from prior knowledge, [U], pricing NOT verified this session**) sit in the middle and
   charge the sub an annual fee for access. You would be selling against a toll booth the customer
   is already forced through. **Probability: high.**

2. **Your best-dated launch event is deregulatory.** NZ HSWA amendments (1 April 2027) narrow
   small-PCBU duties to critical risks and create an ACOP safe harbour. The most credible timed
   catalyst in this memo makes documentation *less* mandatory for sub-20-employee firms.
   **Probability: high, and already certain — the Act has Royal Assent.**

3. **The NSW "Building Commission crackdown" tailwind does not apply to civil.** Every statute in
   the Fair Trading and Building Legislation Amendment Bill 2026 is residential or vertical
   building. If the go-to-market leans on it, the thesis is standing on the wrong sector.
   **Probability: high if used; avoidable.**

4. **The strongest documented software gap has no commencement date.** Queensland explicitly named
   inadequate accounting software when it **paused** PTA phases 3 and 4 on 31 January 2025. A
   product built for that mandate has no launch date and depends on a government that already
   blinked once. **Probability: medium-high.**

5. **Everything genuinely recurring is already owned by an adjacent category.** Quarterly portable
   LSL returns and wage-theft-defensive records → payroll. TPAR → accounting. Retention trusts and
   project trust accounts → accounting. Payment claims → Payapps / Progressclaim-class tools
   **[U, unverified]**. SWMS and inductions → the site-safety incumbents. There is no orphaned
   recurring document in this memo. Every one of them has a landlord. **Probability: medium-high.**

## 10. The one genuine wedge the regulation supports

If something survives, it is this, and it is narrow:

**Multi-jurisdiction divergence with dated re-papering events.** The regulation is not converging,
it is *forking* — Victoria outside WHS entirely and with its own 15 April 2026 SOP model; NSW
re-numbering its entire WHS Regulation in 2025 so every document citing "clause 291" is now stale;
WA at a A$20,000 retention-trust threshold; Queensland's PTA frozen mid-rollout; NZ moving to a
critical-risk duty on 1 April 2027. A civil subcontractor working across two or three states holds
three different document sets, and each fork has a **date** on which existing documents become
wrong.

That is a content-and-currency problem (keep the right document valid in the right jurisdiction on
the right date), not a forms problem. Forms are commodity. **Jurisdictional currency is not.**

## 11. Cheapest next test (next 30 days)

**Test the buyer, not the pain.** Two calls, in this order:

1. **Ten civil subcontractors, A$2m–20m revenue, in two states.** One question set:
   *"In the last 12 months, what did you pay for out of your own bank account so you could get on
   site — name the invoice, the amount, and who sent it."* You are testing whether the sub already
   has a **compliance line item** (a Cm3/Avetta/Rapid/prequal renewal, a HSEQ consultant retainer,
   a safety software subscription) or whether every dollar is really the head contractor's.
   **Stop signal:** if fewer than 5 of 10 can name an invoice they personally paid, the budget is
   upstream and the subcontractor-direct thesis is dead. Repoint at head contractors.

2. **Five NSW head contractors / HSEQ managers, one question:**
   *"Your SWMS templates and WHS management plan cite clauses of the WHS Regulation 2017, which was
   replaced by the WHS Regulation 2025. Have you re-papered? Who is doing it, and what did it cost?"*
   This tests the single cheapest, most concrete, most dated wedge in the memo — a whole state's
   document library going stale — and it does it with a question no incumbent salesperson is asking.
   **Stop signal:** if they shrug and say the regulator does not care about clause references, then
   "jurisdictional currency" is not a felt problem and §10 collapses with it.

**Before either call, resolve the three flagged facts** — they are cheap and two of them cut against
the thesis: (a) the actual commencement date of the NSW WHS Regulation 2025; (b) whether reg 291's
fall-height threshold really changed in July 2026; (c) whether NSW and Victoria in fact have **no**
general licensing regime for civil contractors.
