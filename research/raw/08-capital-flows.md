# 08 — Where the Capital Is Actually Flowing (AU Infrastructure & Energy, 2026–2032)

**Prepared:** 2 September 2026
**Client:** Supply Nation *registered* Indigenous-owned civil business; <A$50k capital; no plant; no track record
**Brief:** Find growing capital flows intersecting mandated Indigenous participation, ranked by reachability from a standing start
**Currency:** All figures A$ unless stated. As-of dates on every figure.

---

## VERDICT

**Go-with-changes — but not to the sector the brief assumed, and one thing must be fixed within weeks or nothing else matters.**

The capital flows are real and the participation mandates are real. But the intersection the brief hypothesised as "the single richest in the country" — transmission — is the *worst* fit for this client on timing, and the sector with the largest Indigenous spend — mining — is the worst fit on structure. The honest ranking puts **Defence estate** and **remote NT housing/essential services** first, because they are the only two places where a mandate gives a business with no plant a *legally protected first look* at a contract it can actually perform. Renewables civil (especially BESS) is third on volume and speed.

The gating issue: on **1 July 2026 the Commonwealth IPP definition changed from 50% Indigenous-owned to 51%+ Indigenous owned *and controlled*** (or ORIC-registered). "Supply Nation registered" is the old 50% standard. Transitional arrangements run through FY2026–27 only. If this client is not **Supply Nation Certified** before 30 June 2027, it loses access to the Commonwealth channel — which is the majority of its realistic revenue. That is not a risk; it is a deadline.

## THE GAP (precisely stated)

**Who:** EPC and delivery contractors on CIS-funded and REZ projects, Defence PDS contractors, and NT remote housing panel builders.

**What they can't do today:** meet a *contractual, reportable* First Nations participation obligation with a supplier who is (a) certified under the **new 51% owned-and-controlled** rule, (b) sized to hold a **$50k–$300k** package, (c) already insured, inducted and safety-systemed, and (d) **in the right postcode at the right time**. The large Indigenous contractors won't mobilise for $80k. The small ones aren't work-ready. The binding constraint is not price — it is *supply of compliant, work-ready Indigenous subcontractors at small package size*.

**What it costs them:** CIS proponents must publish First Nations commitments within 20 days of contract execution and are assessed against them. NSW APIC requires 1.5% of contract value. WA requires targets on ≥$5m construction contracts. Missing these is a reportable contractual failure on a project with public scrutiny.

**The reachable scopes — verbatim from live package lists, not invented:**
- Live transmission project EOI (procurement progressively by work package, contract award expected late Q4 2026): *"site compound establishment, earthworks, ground stabilisations, landscaping, plant and equipment hire, quarry supply, steel reinforcements, fencing, stormwater installation, labour hire, FRP, and environmental services."*
- New England Solar Farm (Green Light EPC): *"Traffic Management, Road Furniture supply and installation, Landscaping, and fencing installation."*
- Broadsound Solar Farm: *"security fencing and site access and maintenance tracks."* Bulli Creek Solar: **51,000m of internal roads**.

**No-plant reachable from that list:** labour hire; fencing (install subcontract); landscaping / revegetation; environmental services (weed and erosion control); traffic management; site compound establishment as a coordinating sub.

**Explicitly NOT reachable — the brief's suggestion of cultural heritage monitoring is a dead end for this client.** Cultural heritage work is gated by *statutory Traditional Owner standing*: in Victoria, Registered Aboriginal Parties under the Aboriginal Heritage Act 2006 are the approving and participating bodies; Powerlink contracts with named Aboriginal Parties within their native title claim areas. An Indigenous business without standing on that country cannot supply the monitors. Do not build a plan on it.

## BOTTOM-UP SIZING (attack these inputs)

**Channel A — Commonwealth MSA (direct contracts, no gatekeeper)**
- FY2024–25 IPP flow: $1.6bn across 13,144 contracts → **average $122k** `[ESTIMATE, derived]`
- Defence ≈ half the cumulative flow; assume ≈ half the annual flow → **~$800m/yr, ~6,500 contracts** `[ESTIMATE]`
- Client wins **4–8 contracts/yr at $80k–$150k** → **$320k–$1.2m revenue**
- At 12–18% net on labour-only scopes `[ESTIMATE — no published margin data found for Indigenous civil-services subcontracting]` → **$40k–$180k net**

**Channel B — REZ/renewables subcontract (gatekept by EPCs)**
- CWO REZ: $5.5bn × 1.5% (NSW APIC) = **~$82m** participation value over the build
- Assume 40% is subcontract spend, rest employment/training `[ESTIMATE]` = **~$33m**, over ~4 years = **~$8m/yr**
- Contested by a subset of the **~6,000 verified businesses on Indigenous Business Direct**
- Client capturing 1–3% → **$80k–$250k/yr**

**Realistic 12-month envelope for a well-executed standing start: A$300k–A$1.2m revenue, A$40k–A$180k net.** That satisfies "cash in 12 months". It does **not** satisfy "a large outcome later" — see the closing section.

## KILL SHOTS (ranked by probability)

1. **Eligibility lapse — near-certain unless acted on. It is a deadline, not a risk.** "Supply Nation registered" is the superseded 50% standard. From 1 July 2026 the IPP requires **51%+ owned and controlled** (or ORIC). Transition ends with FY2026–27. Miss it and the Commonwealth channel — the majority of realistic revenue and the only channel with no gatekeeper — closes. *Fix cost: low. Ignore cost: total.*
2. **Black-cladding backlash tightens the screws again.** ABC (15 June 2026): ~$15bn spent over a decade, "only a fraction" reaching Indigenous people; critics say the July 2026 changes don't go far enough. Expect harder tests of *control*, and hostility to pass-through and front structures — exactly what a no-plant business is tempted to build. Winning work by fronting a non-Indigenous crew puts the client on the wrong side of the next reform.
3. **Traditional Owner primacy caps the upside.** 61–75% of major-miner Indigenous spend is TO-directed; cultural heritage is gated by statutory standing. If the client's country is not where the capital is flowing, the headline Indigenous-spend numbers overstate its addressable market by roughly 3–4x.
4. **Transmission timing mismatch.** HumeLink finishing (2026–27). VNI West completion slipped 2028→2030, approval only expected late 2026, early works late 2026. New England REZ: financial close 2028, construction H2 2027. A business needing cash inside 12 months cannot wait for these. Only **CWO REZ, CopperString Eastern Link and Marinus early works** are live now.
5. **Announced GW ≠ built GW.** Large-scale generation financial close fell to **2.3 GW in 2025 from 4.4 GW in 2024** even as CIS awarded 12 GW across Tenders 7 and 8. Chasing announcements instead of sites with notice-to-proceed is the standard way small subcontractors die.
6. **Working capital, not competition, is what actually kills it.** Labour-supply scopes on 30–60 day terms need roughly **$12k–$20k of standing working capital per $100k of annualised labour revenue** `[ESTIMATE: ~45 days of wages]`. $1m of labour revenue needs $120k–$200k — more than the client has. Growth is the threat.

## CHEAPEST NEXT TEST (30 days, ~$3k)

**Do first, not as a test — it is a gate:** begin **Supply Nation Certification** (51% owned *and* controlled). Nothing below is worth doing without it.

**Then run one procurement-side test.** Do not survey the market; ask the six organisations that currently carry a live, contractual First Nations obligation:
1. **ACEREZ** (CWO REZ, in construction) 2. **UGL CPB JV** (CopperString) 3. **Powerlink** CopperString supplier team 4. **DT Infrastructure / Samsung C&T** (TasVic Greenlink, Marinus Stage 1 civils) 5. **GenusPlus** (Koolunga BESS EPC, SA) 6. **Mott MacDonald / Aurecon / The APP Group** (Defence EWP PDS contractors)

**One question:** *"What package under A$250k do you need an Indigenous subcontractor for in the next two quarters, and what would disqualify us today?"*

In parallel, register on **AusTender** against remote-area categories (this is where all EWP opportunities are published) and lodge EOIs on **ICN Gateway** against the live transmission and VicGrid general EOIs.

**Stop signal.** If **fewer than 3 of 6** name a specific package under $250k with a date — or if 3+ name the disqualifier as *"no plant / no track record / not on our approved subcontractor list and we're not adding"* — then the participation obligations are already being satisfied by incumbents. **Abandon the subcontract strategy.** Fall back to the Commonwealth MSA channel only: remote-area and $80k–$200k direct contracts, where there is no EPC gatekeeper to say no. Smaller, but structurally protected.

## THE "LARGE OUTCOME LATER"

Doing $80k packages forever is not a large outcome. The only credible compounding path visible in this data:

**Use MSA-protected direct Commonwealth contracts (Defence, remote NT) to build cash and a compliance-clean delivery record → then convert that record into a Traditional Owner joint venture on a specific country where a named project is already funded** (CWO REZ, CopperString, Henderson, NT remote housing).

The logic: **61–75% of private-sector Indigenous procurement flows to Traditional Owner businesses.** TO groups hold the standing; many lack delivery capability. A delivery-capable Indigenous partner is the scarce half of that pair, and the Green River Aboriginal Corporation / Big River Housing JV at Peppimenarti is the working template. That is the asset that compounds — not the subcontract book.

**Condition:** any such JV must be structured to survive post-black-cladding *control* tests. Given the June 2026 reporting and the direction of the reforms, a JV that looks like a front will destroy the client's certification and the TO group's reputation together.
