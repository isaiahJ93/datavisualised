## 1. Verdict

**Go-with-changes — and the changes are structural, not tactical.**

The mandates are real, dated, and rising. They are not, in the reachable-with-no-plant part of
the market, *short of supply*. The single biggest reason this fails as currently framed:
**every mandate you are relying on can be satisfied by the buyer without ever giving you a
subcontract.** NSW APIC's 1.5% counts Aboriginal *employment, training and consultation* spend,
not only Aboriginal *business* spend [V]. The Commonwealth's Mandatory Minimum Requirement is
"4% of the workforce **or** 4% of contract value subcontracted" [V] — the head contractor picks
whichever is cheaper, and hiring three Aboriginal labourers is cheaper and lower-risk than
onboarding an unproven subcontractor. Pain in the tier-1 without a forced budget line to you
is a charity, not a market.

What *does* survive: **three direct-award mechanisms that let a buyer hand you a contract
without you competing on past performance.** Commonwealth **Exemption 16** (any value, any
size, simple quote) [V], the **Mandatory Set-Aside** (remote Australia at any value; elsewhere
$80k–$200k GST-inc) [V], and **NSW direct engagement of Aboriginal businesses up to $250,000**
[V1]. Those are the only doors in Australian public procurement that a business with no track
record can walk through, because they are limited approaches, not open tenders. Everything in
your first 12 months should be aimed at exactly those three doors.

And one thing you must fix before anything else: **your "Registered" status is on a cliff.**
The Commonwealth definition changed to 51% First Nations **owned and controlled** on
1 July 2026, with transitional arrangements only through 2026–27 [V]. If the entity sits at
50% with a non-Indigenous partner, it loses Commonwealth IPP eligibility around 1 July 2027.
Fixing that is free and takes days. Not fixing it makes the rest of this memo moot.

### 2.2 The government is hitting its headline target — and that is bad news, not good

You asked for a missed target because a missed target is an opportunity. **The Commonwealth is
not missing the headline IPP target.** It exceeded both the volume and value targets in
FY2024–25 [V1]. There is no panicked buyer with an unfilled quota at the Commonwealth level.

The failure is one layer down, in the **MMR**, and it is a failure of *enforcement*, not of
supply. The ANAO follow-up performance audit (Auditor-General Report No. 40, 2024–25) found:

- **63% of all contracts recorded in the IPP Reporting Solution between July 2016 and
  September 2024 — valued at A$69.3 billion — were exempted from the MMRs by the entities
  themselves.** [V]
- Of 870 MMR contracts commencing in that window, only **161 (18.5%) had a final MMR compliance
  assessment recorded**; of those assessed, 116 (72%) were judged to have met the target. [V]
- NIAA had **not** performed the required five-yearly effectiveness review of the
  procurement-connected policy; the MMR evaluation strategy was finalised but **not
  implemented**; there is **no performance monitoring and limited public reporting** on MMR
  effectiveness. [V]
- The February 2025 reforms were announced **without a clear understanding of the policy's
  effectiveness**. [V]

**Read that as a buyer would.** A rule that is exempted 63% of the time and assessed 18.5% of
the time is not a rule that forces anyone to buy from you. Do not build a revenue model on MMR
compliance pressure.

## 3. The supply gap — and why it is probably not where you were told it is

**Falsification first.** The assignment assumes mandated demand exceeds capable Indigenous
supply. The evidence I could gather points to a **distribution problem, not a scarcity problem**:

- **Concentration.** In the first eight years of the IPP (2015 → mid-2023), 12,800 contracts
  worth **A$7bn** went to 900+ Indigenous firms — but **50% of that value (A$3.5bn) went to
  just 18 businesses**, and **50% of the contract count went to just 11 businesses** (ANU /
  *Australian Journal of Public Administration*, via FOI). [V]
- **Geography.** Canberra-based businesses won **5,272 contracts — over 40% of the count — and
  30% of value (A$2.1bn)**, despite the ACT holding **1% of Indigenous Australians**. [V]
- Businesses in regional and remote Australia are "more likely to be microbusinesses aimed at
  servicing the local community" and face barriers of distance, density and enabling
  infrastructure. [V1]

The reading: buyers are not short of Indigenous suppliers. Buyers are **repeat-buying from a
small set of proven Indigenous suppliers** because that is the lowest-risk way to hit a target.
Ten years of that behaviour has produced incumbents with past performance, prequalification and
balance sheets. Your competition is not "no one". Your competition is 18 firms who already have
the relationships.

**The no-plant categories are the crowded ones.** Search the Indigenous labour and traffic space
and it is thick with established operators: First Nations Traffic Management (VIC since 2019,
NSW since 2023), Moonyah Workforce, Amos Roberts Indigenous Labour Hire, Birli Group (WA
traffic management), YLS (Ballarat/Geelong/Yambuk traffic control), plus the largest Indigenous
VicRoads-prequalified traffic control company running **250+ traffic controllers**. [V1]
In civil delivery: Intract Indigenous Contractors, By Group Aboriginal Civil Construction,
Kirra Services, Indigenous Contractors Australia (QLD). [V1]

**"No plant, no crew" pushes you into precisely the categories with the lowest barriers to
entry — which is why they are already full.** That is the finding. It is not what you wanted.

**Where a gap plausibly remains** (I could not verify category-level supply-vs-demand data —
see §9 — so these are labelled as inference, and §8 tells you how to measure them yourself for
free in 30 days):

1. **Remote and very remote delivery** — the MSA has no dollar floor there, buyers must offer
   first refusal, and the supply base is thin and microbusiness-scale. [inference from V]
2. **Aboriginal cultural heritage monitoring / site officer supply on civil works** — labour and
   coordination, near-zero capital, day-rate billing, and the one category where Indigenous
   ownership is functionally rather than politically relevant. [inference — **unverified**]
3. **Certified (51% owned/managed/controlled) suppliers in regional NSW/QLD** — the certification
   tightening (§4, §5) is about to shrink the eligible pool while targets keep rising. Scarcity
   created by a rule change is the most reliable kind. [inference from V]

What is **not** a gap, on the evidence: metropolitan traffic management, general labour hire,
site cleaning, PPE and consumables resale.

**Sector size, for scale.** Supply Nation's *Sleeping Giant Rises* (2025) reports member
organisations spent **A$5.8bn** with verified Indigenous businesses in FY2024–25 (+A$1.2bn YoY),
**90% of it with SMEs**. By sector: mining A$1.64bn, **construction A$1.26bn**. By state:
NSW A$1.71bn, WA A$1.43bn, ACT A$1.13bn, QLD A$800m, VIC A$630m. [V]

## 4. Registered vs Certified — fix this in week one

| | **Registered** | **Certified** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ownership | **≥50%** Indigenous owned | **≥51%** Indigenous **owned, managed and controlled** |
| Control test | none | key decisions on finance, operations, personnel and strategy rest with an Indigenous Australian |
| Trading history | none evident | **trading evidence of A$50k revenue in the last 12 months, or a demonstrated recent history of trade (ideally ≥6 months)** [V1 — single secondary source, **verify directly with Supply Nation**] |
| Cost | **A$0** | **A$0** — free for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander businesses [V] |
| Process | online form ~30 min; 5-step verification ~4 days | invited by Supply Nation after registration; document review + (from 2026) interview |
| Documents | ABN/ASIC, constitution or partnership agreement or trust deed, share certificates, ownership declarations, **Confirmation of Aboriginality** (Land Council letter or signed confirmation-of-descent form) | as Registered, plus evidence of management and control | [V] |

**Where Certified is required, not merely preferred:**

- **Victoria's Social Procurement Framework** — an Aboriginal business must be **≥51% owned and
  certified by Kinaway or Supply Nation** to count as a social benefit supplier. Registration at
  50% does not count. [V]
- **Commonwealth IPP from 1 July 2026** — the eligibility test is now **51% owned and
  controlled** (or ORIC-registered), with only 2026–27 transitional cover under the old 50%
  test. [V]
- **WA APP** — spend and jobs count only if the Aboriginal partner is on **ABDWA or Supply
  Nation IBD**. [V]
- Most large corporates filter IBD on **Certified** by default. [V1]

**Recommendation, and it is the highest-value line in this memo:** move to **51%+ Indigenous
owned, managed and controlled** and convert Registered → **Certified** before the transition
closes. It costs A$0 and days of paperwork. It removes a cliff dated roughly **1 July 2027**,
and it unlocks Victoria and the corporate filter. If the entity is already 100% Indigenous
owned and controlled, the only blocker is the trading-history test — which is one more reason
to bank a small first contract fast (§7).

**Verification is now real-time.** Supply Nation takes a **daily ASIC feed** and audits ownership
changes against it. [V] Do not sign anything that quietly hands control to a non-Indigenous
party — it will be seen.

## 5. Black cladding and the integrity reforms — a constraint, not a risk to route around

The reforms are moving fast and they are aimed squarely at the shortcut a capital-poor entrant
is most tempted to take.

- **Definition (Supply Nation):** a non-Indigenous entity or shareholder taking unfair advantage
  of an Indigenous business entity to access Indigenous procurement policies or contracts, in a
  way that disadvantages or is detrimental to the Indigenous business. [V]
- **Indigenous Business Integrity Register (IBIR)** — launched **October 2025**; supports
  certification work using AI plus **external Certified Practising Accountants and auditors**
  managing verification integrity. [V1]
- **From 2026: every Supply Nation Certified Supplier must complete a 30-minute, face-to-face
  re-certification interview** with an Indigenous Business Officer, verifying ownership,
  management and control, and collecting revenue and geographic data. [V1]
- **Enforcement:** on complaint, Supply Nation investigates under its Supplier Terms &
  Conditions and Code of Conduct; where a business is found to be black-clad, **it takes action
  against the business**. [V] Marketing yourself as Registered/Certified while not continuously
  meeting the requirements is itself characterised as black cladding. [V]
- **Political heat:** ABC News ran "Multi-billion-dollar economic policy undermined by 'black
  cladding'" on **15 June 2026**, with critics saying the 1 July changes would not go far
  enough. NIBCA publicly welcomed stronger Supply Nation checks (Nov 2025). [V]

**Joint ventures.** Supply Nation applies **separate, stricter criteria** to Indigenous joint
ventures than to ordinary businesses, designed to ensure the JV is a **genuine pooling of both
parties' resources, skills and assets** with **measures in place to build the capacity of the
Indigenous JV party**. [V] Translation: a JV where the non-Indigenous partner supplies the plant,
the crew, the systems and the management while you supply the badge is the textbook case the
criteria exist to catch. A JV where you hold 51%, control the decisions, and there is a written,
evidenced capability-transfer plan is legitimate and is a reasonable way to solve the plant
problem — but the paperwork has to be true, and from 2026 someone will sit across a table and
ask you about it for 30 minutes.

**What "genuine delivery capability" looks like in practice** [inference — no primary source
obtained]: your own ABN holding the contract; your own insurances and workers' compensation;
your own supervisors on site; your own SWMS and ITPs in your own document set; your people on
your payroll; margin retained in your entity rather than passed through to a partner at cost.
If the answer to "what do you actually do that the other party doesn't?" is "we invoice", you
are exposed.

sell direct, sell often.**

## 7. The realistic first contract — and what is not reachable

### 7.1 Not reachable from here. Say it plainly.

- **Tier-1 civil subcontract packages.** These need prequalification, a safety-management system
  audit, a track record, insurances at project limits, and the working capital to carry 30–60 day
  progress claims with 5% retention held to practical completion and 2.5% through the defects
  period [U]. Under A$50k you cannot fund one crew for one month on those terms. **Not in
  year one.**
- **Anything requiring plant.** Earthworks, drainage, bulk excavation, pavement. Wet-hire margins
  do not survive if you are also renting the plant.
- **Head-contract tenders on AusTender or ProcurePoint open ATMs.** You will be scored on past
  performance you do not have, against 18 firms who do.
- **Remote NT/WA delivery in the next 6 months.** The regulatory geography is the best in the
  country and the mobilisation cost is the worst. Reachable via partnering, not directly.
- **Bonded or panel-based work.** Panels were the single barrier First Nations businesses named
  in the NIAA IPP reform consultations [V1]. Do not spend your first 6 months on panel
  applications.

### 7.2 What is reachable in 6 months

**The shape of the first contract:** a labour- or coordination-intensive scope, billed on day
rates or unit rates against a purchase order, **direct to a government buyer under Exemption 16,
the MSA, or NSW's $250k direct engagement**, in the A$15k–$120k range, with monthly invoicing
and no retention. Not a subcontract. Not a tender.

**Steps, in order:**

1. **Week 1 — fix the structure.** Get to 51%+ Indigenous owned, managed and controlled. Apply
   for **Supply Nation Certification** (free; ~4-day verification once documents are in).
   Confirm the trading-history requirement directly with Supply Nation before you assume you
   qualify — this is [V1] and it is load-bearing.
   https://supplynation.org.au/benefits/supplier/
2. **Week 1 — get listed everywhere a buyer looks.**
   - **AusTender** supplier profile, free, with the Indigenous flag set — this is how
     Commonwealth buyers find MSA and Exemption 16 candidates. The Supplier Portal opened to all
     businesses from 1 July 2026. https://www.tenders.gov.au/
   - **Supply Nation Indigenous Business Direct** (6,400+ verified businesses as at July 2026 —
     assume you are competing for attention, so write the profile like a capability statement,
     not a bio). https://supplynation.org.au/
   - **buy.nsw / ProcurePoint** (NSW): https://www.procurepoint.nsw.gov.au/
   - **ABDWA** (WA — required for WA APP spend to count)
   - **Kinaway** (VIC — required for Victorian SPF): https://www.kinaway.com.au/
   - **NTIBN** (NT): https://ntibn.com.au/
   - **ICN Gateway** — where head contractors post project work packages:
     https://gateway.icn.org.au/
3. **Weeks 2–6 — free capability support, in this order:**
   - **NSWICC** — peak body, free advisory services in Sydney metro/Greater Sydney, delivered by
     Aboriginal advisers; **engaged by Transport for NSW specifically to help Aboriginal
     businesses build tender responses and join TfNSW construction prequalification panels and
     schemes**. Assured Membership requires ≥51% Indigenous owned and controlled — another
     reason to fix the structure first. https://nswicc.com.au/business-programs/transport-prequalification/
   - **Indigenous Business Australia** — free workshops (procurement, business planning,
     finance); **loans up to A$500,000 at 5.5% fixed for 5 years** for businesses ≥50%
     Indigenous owned, and a start-up finance package reportedly awarding **up to 30% of a new
     business loan as a grant** for asset purchase [V1 — secondary source, **verify with IBA
     directly**]; Small Business Development Program (structured support up to 3 years).
     This is your realistic answer to "no plant" — asset finance at 5.5%, not a JV that risks
     your certification. https://iba.gov.au/
   - **business.gov.au** Indigenous business advice listings.
4. **Weeks 2–12 — go get the three doors.**
   - Identify Commonwealth entities with a **regional/remote footprint and a construction or
     facilities-maintenance spend**, and contact their procurement teams directly citing
     **Exemption 16** and the **MSA** by name. You are not asking for a favour; you are pointing
     at a rule that says they must consider you first.
   - In NSW, target buyers under the **A$250,000 direct-engagement** ceiling.
   - Frame every approach around the **volume target** (3% of contract *count*), not value.
     A buyer three contracts short of a count target will take a $20k contract from you.
5. **Months 3–9 — subcontract to established Indigenous primes, not to tier-1s.**
   Intract, By Group, Kirra Services and similar already hold the head contracts and carry the
   flow-down obligations, and have materially less prequalification friction than CPB or John
   Holland [inference — unverified]. This is the fastest route to a track record.
6. **Events.** **Supply Nation Connect 2026 was 11–12 August 2026 at the Perth Convention &
   Exhibition Centre and the exhibitor floor sold out** [V] — you have missed it by three weeks.
   Diarise **Connect 2027** now and book the moment registration opens; budget for a tradeshow
   booth as the single largest marketing line you will have. State chambers (NSWICC, Kinaway,
   NTIBN) run "meet the buyer" sessions between Connects at far lower cost.

- FY2024–25: **13,144** Indigenous contracts nationally [V1].
- Civil-construction-adjacent share of that count: **8–15%** [ESTIMATE — construction is 1 of
  19 MMR sectors; Supply Nation members put construction at A$1.26bn of A$5.8bn = 21.7% by
  value, but the Commonwealth mix is more services-weighted]. Take 10% → **~1,300 contracts/yr**.
- One state at ~25% of count [ESTIMATE] → **~325 addressable contracts/yr**.
- A no-track-record entrant winning **2–5** of them in year one at **A$20k–$120k** each →
  **A$60k–A$400k revenue**.
- Labour-based civil services gross margin **20–30%** [U] → **A$15k–A$100k gross profit**.

**Path B — the tier-1 / Indigenous-prime supply chain at maturity.**
- Supply Nation member spend with Indigenous businesses in **construction: A$1.26bn** FY2024–25 [V].
- Indigenous construction businesses on IBD: **~960** [ESTIMATE — 15% of 6,400 verified].
- Naive average: **A$1.3m per construction supplier per year**.
- Apply the observed concentration (≈50% of value to ≈2% of firms) → the other ~95% of firms
  share roughly **A$630m across ~910 firms ≈ A$690k each** [ESTIMATE].
- Year-one capture for a no-plant, no-record entrant: **5–15% of that median → A$35k–A$105k.**
  Reaching the A$690k median is a **2–4 year** trajectory, and requires plant or a genuine
  51%-controlled JV to get there.

**Honest conclusion:** **A$60k–A$300k of revenue in the first 12 months is achievable via direct
awards.** A large outcome later is achievable but runs through **plant finance (IBA at 5.5%),
certification, and a repeat-buyer relationship** — not through the mandates getting more
generous.

## 8. Kill shots, ranked by probability

1. **The 51%-owned-and-controlled cliff (high, and dated).** Commonwealth eligibility became
   51% owned **and controlled** on 1 July 2026; transitional cover runs only through 2026–27
   [V]. If the entity is 50/50 with a non-Indigenous partner, it loses IPP eligibility around
   **1 July 2027** and is already ineligible in Victoria today. **Cost to fix: A$0 and a
   shareholders' agreement. Fix it this month.**
2. **Working capital, not demand, ends this (high).** Civil subcontracting means progress claims
   on 30–60 day terms, 5% retention to practical completion, 2.5% through defects, plus
   insurances and prequalification costs [U]. Under A$50k cannot carry a crew through one cycle.
   **Mitigation: refuse subcontract terms in year one. Take purchase-order, day-rate, monthly-billed,
   retention-free work direct from government buyers.**
3. **Buyers satisfy mandates without buying from you (high).** APIC's 1.5% counts employment,
   training and consultation [V]; MMR is 4% employment **or** 4% subcontracting [V]; WA APRs are
   employment **or** subcontracting [V]. And the MMR is exempted 63% of the time and assessed
   18.5% of the time [V]. **Any pitch built on "you have to buy from me" is false and buyers
   know it.**
4. **Incumbent concentration and past-performance gates (high).** 18 firms took 50% of A$7bn;
   Canberra took 40% of contract count [V]. Buyers de-risk by repeat-buying.
   **Mitigation: chase the count target with small contracts the incumbents don't want.**
5. **Category saturation in every no-plant category (medium-high).** Traffic management, labour
   hire and site services are already dense with established Indigenous operators [V1].
   **Mitigation: pick a narrower scope and prove it with §9's test before committing.**
6. **Black-cladding exposure via the plant workaround (medium probability, terminal
   consequence).** The obvious fix for "no plant" is a JV with a non-Indigenous civil
   contractor. Supply Nation applies stricter JV criteria requiring genuine pooling of resources
   and evidenced capability building [V]; the IBIR (Oct 2025), the daily ASIC feed, and the
   mandatory 30-minute face-to-face re-certification interview from 2026 exist to find exactly
   this [V/V1]. **Removal from IBD ends the business.** If you do a JV, hold 51%, control the
   decisions, and paper the capability transfer honestly.

## 10. Cheapest next test — 30 days, under A$500

Three tests. Run them in parallel. Two are free.

**Test 1 — Count the supply side yourself (free, 4 hours).**
Open Indigenous Business Direct. For your state, filter **Certified** suppliers in each candidate
category: traffic management, labour hire, erosion and sediment control, fencing, cultural
heritage monitoring, revegetation and landscape maintenance, site services. **Count them.**
- **Stop signal:** more than ~15 Certified suppliers already in your state in a category = the
  mandate is not scarce there. Cross it off.
- **Go signal:** fewer than 5 Certified suppliers in a category with live demand = that is your
  wedge. Repeat for ABDWA (WA) and Kinaway (VIC).

**Test 2 — One question to ten Indigenous Participation Managers (free, 10 calls).**
Ring the Indigenous Participation Advisor/Manager at ten head contractors and state agencies and
ask exactly one question:
> *"In the last financial year, how many dollars of your APIC / MMR / APR obligation did you
> meet through **employment and training** rather than through Indigenous **business** spend —
> and was that a choice or because you couldn't find a supplier?"*
- **Stop signal:** they overwhelmingly say "employment, by choice, it's simpler". Then the
  mandate does not create subcontract demand and the thesis is dead in its current form.
- **Go signal:** two or more say "we couldn't find a supplier for X" and name the same X. That
  is your category and your first customer in one sentence.

**Test 3 — Ten direct-award approaches (free, 2 weeks).**
Identify ten Commonwealth and NSW buyers with regional construction or facilities spend. Email
each a one-page capability statement citing **Exemption 16**, the **Mandatory Set-Aside**, and
(for NSW) the **A$250,000 direct-engagement** provision by name, offering a A$20k–$80k scope.
- **Stop signal:** zero requests for a quote from ten approaches. If nobody will even ask you to
  price a job you can deliver with no plant, the direct-award door is not open to you, and
  nothing else in this memo works either.
- **Go signal:** two or more quote requests. That converts to your first contract and to the
  trading evidence that unlocks Certification.

**And regardless of outcome, this month: restructure to 51% owned/managed/controlled and lodge
the Certification application.** It is free, it takes days, and the transition window closes
around 1 July 2027.
