## Verdict

**No-go on the obvious version of this idea; go-with-major-changes on a narrower one.**

The single biggest reason: **the "one profile, many head contractors" pitch is already
the incumbent's own marketing copy, and the free-to-subcontractor model is already
funded to US$70M.** Cm3 — the largest ANZ player — sells itself on *"one fee, for one
year, for unlimited clients"* ([Cm3 contractor FAQ, via search synthesis, accessed 1 Sep
2026](https://www.cm3.com.au/contractors/faq/)) [A]. HammerTech, with **US$70M from
Riverwood Capital (Aug 2023)**, is **free for subcontractors — "Subcontractors do not
need to purchase a license"** ([HammerTech pricing](https://www.hammertech.com/en-us/pricing))
[A]. ComplyFlow is **"free for contractors and suppliers"**
([complyflow.com](https://www.complyflow.com/)) [A]. And the single most important
prequalification in Australian civil — the Austroads **National Prequalification System
for Civil (Road and Bridge) Construction** — states plainly: **"There is no fee to
apply."** ([Austroads](https://austroads.gov.au/infrastructure/national-prequalification))
[A].

So the resentment is real and I found it verbatim. But the *arbitrage* is not there:
the party with the pain (the sub) has no budget, the party with the budget (the
principal) is being courted by four PE-funded platforms who already give the sub a free
seat, and in the highest-value civil segment the state road agencies have already made
prequalification free. What survives is a much narrower thesis — see **The gap**.

### F2. Who pays: the split is genuinely bimodal, and the "sub pays" side is the *older* half.

**Sub pays (the compliance-tax cohort):**

- **Cm3** — contractor pays. **A$399–A$3,045 per year ex GST**, tiered by the contractor's
  *Business Risk Profile* (BRP). Marketing line: *"one fee, for one year, for unlimited
  clients."* Claims >half of contractors serve 2+ clients on one subscription.
  [Cm3 contractor FAQ](https://www.cm3.com.au/contractors/faq/) [A, via synthesis]
- **Conserve** — **A$240.00 + GST annual licensing fee payable by the supplier**. This is
  the cleanest primary evidence I found, because it appears in a *council's* contractor
  information sheet, not vendor marketing: [City of Canada Bay contractor information
  sheet](https://hdp-au-prod-app-cbay-collaborate-files.s3.ap-southeast-2.amazonaws.com/6216/0816/4005/Information_sheet_Conserve_Contractor_System_for_Contractors.pdf)
  [A]. Conserve positions itself as "the most affordable contractor management
  application available in Australia" ([conserve.com.au](https://www.conserve.com.au/contractor-faqs)).
- **Rapid Global** — **A$25.00 per induction**, registration payable **once per annum**;
  *"if a company is being paid as a sub-contractor under their own ABN, the inductee will
  need to register their own induction key"* — i.e. the fee lands on the sub, per worker.
  [Rapid Global FAQ (hosted by client GPCL)](https://gpcl.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Rapid-Global-FAQs-Contact-Company-Admin.pdf);
  [Rapid Global user guide](https://gpcl.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Rapid-Global-User-Guide.pdf) [A]
- **Avetta** — supplier pays an annual subscription, tiered by number of client
  connections, region, size. **No AUD figure published; not found.** US/global
  triangulation: **US$450–900/yr** basic, **>US$1,200** with analytics/training
  ([crewcompliance](https://crewcompliance.org/blog/avetta-certification-cost),
  [expiryedge](https://expiryedge.com/articles/avetta-vs-isnetworld-comparison/)) [C].
  Avetta's own AU plans page exists but was unfetchable:
  [avetta.com/en-au/suppliers-contractors/plans](https://www.avetta.com/en-au/suppliers-contractors/plans).
- **Rail Industry Worker (RIW)** — **A$130.00 per worker per year** for organisations with
  <299 cardholders, up from A$120 on 1 Jul 2022, **plus** card supply and ID-check fees.
  Volume discounts above 299 cards. This is the one that actually bites civil rail subs.
  [RIW fee increase notice](https://www.riw.net.au/news-20220609-annual-riw-card-fee-increase/);
  [RIW pricing model](https://riw.net.au/about-us/pricing-and-fees) [A] — **note: 2022
  figure, almost certainly higher in 2026; current rate not found.**
- **SiteWise (NZ, Site Safe)** — **from NZ$250 + GST/yr**, contractor pays; 3 assessment
  attempts year one, 1 thereafter, extra attempts **NZ$100 + GST**.
  [sitewise.co.nz/pricing](https://sitewise.co.nz/pricing/) [A]
- **IMPAC PREQUAL / Tōtika-aligned (NZ)** — contractor pays, tiered:
  **NZ$298 + GST** (sole trader–20 staff, contracts ≤NZ$2.5M) · **NZ$598 + GST** (≤200
  staff, ≤NZ$20M) · **NZ$999 + GST** (>200 staff, >NZ$20M, very-high-risk work).
  [impac.co.nz/prequal](https://impac.co.nz/prequal) [A]
- **PREQUAL (prequal.co.nz)** — explicitly dual-sided: *"Clients pay an annual fee to
  access the PREQUAL database (determined by the number of contractors they typically
  use), while contractors pay a prequalification assessment fee based on their size and
  category."* [prequal.co.nz FAQs](https://www.prequal.co.nz/faqs) [A]
- **Sitepass (Quartex)** — hybrid by design. Support docs state administrators of a Client
  or Enterprise account **"can put the onus of paying the contractor connection fee on
  their contractors."** Fee amount **not found** (described only as "relatively
  inexpensive"; enterprise clients can ask support to change it).
  [Sitepass connection fee overview](https://support.mysitepass.com/en/articles/337-contractor-connection-fee-overview) [A]
- **LinkSafe** — hybrid, three explicit options: contractor pays per annum via LinkSafe's
  payment gateway; LinkSafe invoices the *client* monthly per prequalified contractor; or
  a fixed per-annum client fee. Separately: *"some LinkSafe clients charge a fee to
  workers to undertake their induction."*
  [LinkSafe Concierge](https://linksafe.com.au/solutions/linksafe-concierge/) [A]

**Principal/head contractor pays (the free-to-sub cohort):**

- **HammerTech** — *"HammerTech is free for subcontractors. Subcontractors do not need to
  purchase a license."* Flat fee, unlimited users, GC pays.
  [hammertech.com/en-us/pricing](https://www.hammertech.com/en-us/pricing) [A]
- **ComplyFlow** — *"free for contractors and suppliers (without linked Clients)"*;

### F3. The compliance tax is real, and I found it stated in the operators' own words.

Verbatim, from a Capterra-verified review of Avetta by a Facilities Services Director:

> **"Our contracts with customers are often fully executed before we discover that we are
> forced to pay for Avetta"** … describing the model as a **"sneaky 'free service' to our
> customers, and 'must comply with us to continue business' extortion."**
> — surfaced via search of [Capterra Avetta reviews](https://capterra.com/p/145741/AuditGUARD/reviews/) [C-transcription of A-source]

Corroborating pattern on the same platform: **85% negative sentiment across 59 Capterra
user reviews**; *"Multiple users on Software Advice complained about being automatically
charged exorbitant fees for unwanted client connections"*; *"many suppliers describe
onboarding as confusing, repetitive, and difficult to cancel or downgrade."*
([G2 Avetta reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/avetta-avetta/reviews),
[SelectHub](https://www.selecthub.com/p/supply-chain-risk-management-software/avetta/)) [C]

On Cm3, the recurring complaint is narrower and softer — cost incidence on small subs,
not conduct:

> **"The cost of the software can be expensive for subcontracts to pay if they are a
> minimal company."** — [Capterra Cm3 reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/218590/Cm3/reviews/) [C]

Plus operational gripes: *"Expired and dormant contractors should be removed from the
screens and sent to an archived system"*; *"little hiccups and mix ups in variables with
ABNs"*; *"operators sometimes over complicate and misinterpret questions asked."* [C]

Structural confirmation that duplication is the norm, from **two competitors' own
marketing** (which is the strongest kind of admission — they are conceding a problem they
must then claim to solve):

- Felix: non-standardised prequalification *"can create huge inefficiencies for all
  parties involved"* — [felix.net](https://www.felix.net/blog/why-standardised-prequalification) [A]
- Tōtika/CHASNZ: built *"in response to contractors repeating the same prequalification
  process for every client, each with differing expectations, platforms, and
  documentation requirements"*; objective is *"a fair and equitable system where
  contractors only have to carry out pre-qualification once for all buyers of their
  services."* — [totika.org](https://www.totika.org/); [CHASNZ](https://chasnz.org/totika) [A]
- Industry commentary: *"Contractors need active profiles in multiple platforms including
  Avetta, ISN, Cm3, Pegasus, and Felix with continuous upkeep of certificates and
  records… there is no mutual recognition between major clients."* [C]
- Australian Constructors Association: *"Inconsistent prequalification and
  expression-of-interest (EOI) processes create duplication and additional work before
  tendering begins."* [ACA publications](https://www.constructors.com.au/publications/);
  [Inside Construction](https://www.insideconstruction.com.au/improving-procurement-to-lift-construction-productivity) [A/B]

### F4. Quantifying the tax — bottom-up, and smaller than the founder probably hopes.

Secondary [C] sources put it at *"Cm3 fees range from approximately $600 to $2,000 per
year"* and *"For a sole trader earning $80,000–$120,000 per year, the combined cost of
multiple prequalification subscriptions can represent 2–4% of gross revenue"*
([EHS Atlas NSW prequalification guide](https://ehsatlas.ohconsultant.com.au/ehsatlas/compliance/prequalification-guide),
[Built Simple](https://builtsimple.com.au/blog/subcontractor-management-software-australia/)).
**Both are SEO content marketing. Do not quote them to an investor.** The 2–4% figure is
also arithmetically strained: 2% of A$80,000 is A$1,600, which is one Cm3 subscription,
not "multiple."

My own bottom-up build, **[ESTIMATE]**, for a *mid-size ANZ civil sub — 15 field workers,
working across road, rail and water for 4–5 different principals*, using only the cited
unit prices above:

| Line | Basis | Annual A$ |
|---|---|---|
| Cm3 | mid-tier BRP, 1 fee unlimited clients [A: A$399–3,045 band] | 700 |
| Avetta | US$450–900 converted, unverified for AU [C] | 1,000 |
| Conserve | A$240 + GST [A] | 240 |
| Rapid Global inductions | 15 workers × A$25 [A] | 375 |
| RIW cards (rail work) | 15 × A$130 (2022 rate; now higher) [A] | 1,950 |
| LinkSafe / Sitepass / client-specific portals | not found; placeholder | 500 |
| **Direct platform fees** | | **≈ A$4,765** |
| Admin labour re-uploading the same insurances/SWMS/licences | [ESTIMATE] 40–80 hrs @ ~A$45/hr loaded | 1,800–3,600 |
| **All-in compliance tax** | | **≈ A$6,500–8,400** |

**Number of platforms a typical civil sub sits on: 3–5** — this is an **[ESTIMATE]**
inferred from (a) the named-platform lists in industry commentary (Avetta, ISN, Cm3,
Pegasus, Felix), (b) the NSW guide naming Cm3 + Avetta + Veriforce as the main NSW
principal-side platforms, and (c) rail adding RIW on top. **I could not find a survey,
association submission, or inquiry evidence that counts this directly. That number is
the most important unverified input in this memo.**

**Crucially: the cash component is dominated by RIW (per-worker, unavoidable, rail
regulator-adjacent) — which a document-management startup cannot displace.** Strip RIW
out and the addressable annoyance is ~A$2,800 of fees plus ~A$2,700 of admin time. That
is the real willingness-to-pay ceiling, and it is shared across 4–5 vendors who each
already have the relationship.

## The gap — precisely stated

**Who:** the ANZ civil subcontractor of 5–50 people (earthworks, drainage, traffic,
survey, plant hire, line marking, fencing, concrete) that works for 4+ different
principals — Tier-1 civils, state road agencies, water authorities, and rail.

**What they can't do today:** hold **one canonical, always-current evidence set** — public
liability and works insurance, workers comp, plant/vehicle registrations, HRW licences,
VOCs, RIW/white cards, SWMS, ITPs, ISO certs, SDSs — and **push** it, in each recipient's
required shape, into Cm3, Avetta, Rapid, LinkSafe, Sitepass, PREQUAL/SiteWise, the
principal's bespoke SharePoint, and the tender portal, with expiry alerts that fire
before a site access card gets suspended. Today the sub keeps this in a folder and a
spreadsheet and re-keys it 4–8 times a year, per platform, per renewal.

**What it costs them:** **≈A$6,500–8,400/yr all-in [ESTIMATE, F4]**, of which
**≈A$1,800–3,600 is admin labour** — the only part a document tool can actually recover,
because the platform fees themselves are mandated by the principal and will not go away.

**The honest framing:** this is a **time-and-anxiety product for the sub, priced against
~A$2–3k of recoverable admin**, or a **data-plumbing product sold to the principal**. It
is not a "kill the compliance tax" product. Nobody can kill the compliance tax except the
principals, and they don't pay it.

## Kill shots — ranked by probability

1. **[HIGH] The wedge is already taken from both ends.** Cm3 sells "one fee, unlimited
   clients"; HammerTech and ComplyFlow give subs a free seat; Felix sells standardised
   prequal to principals; Tōtika does mutual recognition as a non-profit. A new entrant
   arrives with the same sentence in its pitch deck and no distribution.
2. **[HIGH] The payer has no budget and the budget-holder has no pain.** Hermes rule 4.
   Subs feel it; principals fund it. Every ANZ platform that scaled did so by selling to
   the principal and *back-charging* the sub. A sub-side subscription competes with
   ~A$2–3k of recoverable admin time for a business owner who does the paperwork at
   9pm and values that hour at zero on the P&L.
3. **[HIGH] You cannot integrate your way in.** The moat isn't the document store — it's
   the write path into Cm3/Avetta/Rapid/LinkSafe. None of them has a public partner API
   for third-party evidence push, and all four are now PE-owned with an incentive to keep
   the sub's data captive. Absent an API you are selling a **form-filling assistant**,
   and browser automation against four hostile portals is a support nightmare, not a
   product.
4. **[MEDIUM-HIGH] The biggest cash line is untouchable.** RIW at A$130/worker/yr
   (2022 rate) dominates the rail sub's bill and is a national competency framework, not
   a document store. Water and road authorities are moving the same way. You cannot
   disintermediate a card scheme with a Dropbox.
5. **[MEDIUM] Consolidation eats the white space before you ship.** Four PE houses,
   Autodesk, and Riverwood have all bought in since 2021, and Achilles/Bridgepoint is
   mid-acquisition of Cm3 *right now*. Whatever adjacency you find, one of them buys or
   builds it inside 18 months — and Achilles is buying Cm3 precisely to bolt it onto a
   global supplier-assurance network.
6. **[MEDIUM] Damstra is the comp, and it went for 24 cents.** A$30.3M revenue,
   negative free cash flow, delisted Apr 2024. The category's public-market verdict is
   already in.

## Cheapest next test (next 30 days)

**Resolve the one number this memo could not: how many platforms does a real ANZ civil
sub actually pay for, and what would they pay to stop?**

1. **Twenty structured calls, 20 minutes each** — owners/admins at 5–50-person civil subs
   (earthworks, traffic, drainage, plant hire) in NSW/VIC/QLD + 5 in NZ. Recruit through
   CCF state branches, Civil Contractors NZ, and plant-hire Facebook groups. Ask exactly
   three things: *(a) list every portal you pay for and the invoice amount;* *(b) how many
   hours last month went to uploading documents you'd already uploaded elsewhere;*
   *(c) if a tool cut that to zero, what would you pay per month — and would you sign
   today?* **Cost: ~A$1,500 in incentives + one week.**
2. **In parallel, ask five Tier-1 civil HSEQ managers one question:** *"If your subs kept
   their evidence in a third-party vault, would you accept a feed from it, or must it be
   keyed into your platform?"* Their answer is the entire integration thesis.
3. **Read the channels I couldn't reach:** r/AusConstruction, Whirlpool, HotCopper,
   ProductReview.com.au, and the CCF/MBA member newsletters, for the verbatim rant.

**Stop conditions — any one of these and kill it:**

- Median sub names **≤2 paid platforms** (the "stack" thesis is a myth), **or**
- Median stated willingness-to-pay is **<A$40/month** (below CAC for a 5-person business
  reached by field sales), **or**
- **≥3 of 5** principals say evidence must be keyed into *their* platform and they will
  not accept a third-party feed (no integration path → no product), **or**
- More than half the subs say their principal's platform is **already free to them**
  (the HammerTech/ComplyFlow model has already won the segment).

**Go condition:** median 4+ paid platforms, median WTP ≥A$99/month, and at least two
principals willing to pilot an inbound evidence feed. Only then is there a business — and
even then, sell it to the principal.
