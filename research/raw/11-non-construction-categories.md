## 1. Verdict

**Go-with-changes — but the change is severe, and it is about what you are selling, not where.**

The mandate is real and growing. It is also **oversubscribed on the supply side**. The single
most important number I found: Supply Nation lists **more than 6,000 verified Indigenous
businesses** [V1, Oct 2025], while **just over 1,200 Indigenous businesses won any Commonwealth
contract at all in FY2024-25** [V]. Roughly **four in five registered Indigenous businesses won
nothing from the Commonwealth last year** [ESTIMATE — the two populations are not identical;
see §5]. Indigenous status is not a moat. It is a queue ticket, and the queue is long.

What is *not* oversubscribed is the specific procedural window where your status is worth the
most money to the buyer: **contracts between A$80,000 and A$200,000**, where Exemption 16
removes the buyer's open-tender obligation *and* the Mandatory Set-Aside obliges them to come
to you first. Below $80,000 your status buys you almost no procedural advantage — the buyer
could have directly engaged anyone (§3). Above $200,000 you have no past performance and the
delegate carries personal audit risk (§8, kill shot 2).

So: aim at $80k–$200k, in a category with **zero inventory, zero wages, and milestone billing**,
and treat your first three contracts as purchases of *references*, not revenue. Cash inside 12
months is achievable. A "large outcome" inside 12 months is not, and any adviser telling you
otherwise is selling you something.

## 3. Exemption 16 and the set-aside, in practice — including what it is *not*

### 3.1 The mechanics [V]

- **Exemption 16** (Commonwealth Procurement Rules, Appendix A) lets a relevant Commonwealth
  entity procure **directly from an Indigenous SME at any size and value**, without applying the
  Division 2 rules (i.e. **no open tender**). Value for money still must be demonstrated, but
  **"through a simple quote process"**.
- **Mandatory Set-Aside (MSA)**: an Indigenous business **must be approached first** where the
  contract is (a) **valued A$80,000–A$200,000 GST-inclusive** in a region with a significant
  Indigenous population, **or** (b) **delivered in a remote area, at any value**.
- **From 1 July 2026** eligibility tightened: **51% or more Indigenous owned *and controlled***,
  or ORIC-registered. **FY2026-27 is a transition year** in which either the old 50% test or the
  new 51% test qualifies you [V].

### 3.2 The falsification — where Exemption 16 is worth nothing

Exemption 16 exempts you from **Division 2**, which is the open-tender machinery that only
engages **above the CPR procurement threshold — A$80,000 for non-corporate entities** [U,
corroborated: the MSA band starts at exactly $80,000, which is not a coincidence].

**Therefore: below A$80,000, Exemption 16 confers no procedural advantage whatsoever**, because
below that threshold the buyer could have directly engaged *any* supplier, Indigenous or not.
Your only advantage in that band is the volume-target incentive (§2.2) — real, but soft.

**Above A$80,000, Exemption 16 is genuinely large**: it saves the buyer a 6–12 week open tender
and an evaluation panel. That is worth real money to a time-poor contract manager. **There is
no stated upper limit.**

**Conclusion: your commercial sweet spot is A$80,000–A$200,000** — the band where Exemption 16
removes the tender *and* the MSA compels the first approach. Price and scope your offers to land
there deliberately. A A$45,000 proposal throws away your best asset.

### 3.5 The thing that could lock you out entirely

Supply Nation registration and certification are **free** [V1] — but eligibility requires
**"trading with a minimum annual revenue of A$50,000, or a demonstrated recent history of trade
(ideally at least 6 months)"** [V, two independent results]. Certification additionally requires
**"trading evidence of $50k revenue in the last 12 months"** [V1].

You are described as **barely trading**. If IBD is the primary discovery surface (§3.4) and you
cannot satisfy the trading test, **Exemption 16 is commercially worthless to you** — a buyer
cannot use a supplier they cannot find. This is kill shot #1 and it costs one email to resolve.

**Also note: you are *registered*, not *certified*.** Registered = 50%+ owned. Certified = 51%+
owned, **managed and controlled** [V]. Many corporate buyers specify *certified*. And the
1 July 2026 IPP change moves the Commonwealth to the same 51%-owned-and-controlled standard.
**Upgrading Registered → Certified is free, aligns you to the new statutory test, and unlocks
corporate buyers. Do it first, before anything else in this memo.**

## 6. The panels — and why, for you, they mostly do not matter

This is the most useful mechanical finding in the memo, and it inverts the brief's assumption.

### 6.1 The Flexibility Framework — the door that bypasses the panel

**Both the Management Advisory Services (MAS) Panel and the People Panel operate a Flexibility
Framework**, under which entities may buy a proportion of in-scope services from suppliers
**outside** the arrangement. And specifically:

> Entities can access an **additional A$50,000 or 5 per cent allowance (whichever is greater)
> specifically to engage First Nations businesses** … designed to provide opportunities for new
> market entrants, specialist, niche service providers, **including First Nations businesses
> operating off-panel**. [V1 — Department of Finance MAS FAQ, quoted twice in results]

**What this means in plain terms:** for management advisory and for recruitment/labour-hire
services, **an agency does not need you on the panel to engage you.** The path is:
agency wants the work → uses the First Nations flexibility allowance to go off-panel →
uses Exemption 16 to skip open tender → direct engagement. **No panel. No tender.**

This is the single most actionable mechanic available to you, and it is not widely understood
by suppliers. It should be the opening line of your outreach to agency contract managers,
because **it tells the buyer how to buy from you legally without doing any work.** Buyers do not
say no because they dislike you; they say no because the compliant path is unclear.

**[V1 — single source. Confirm against the MAS Panel User Guide (July 2026) and People Panel
Phase 1/2/3 User Guides before you build outreach on it.]**

## 8. Kill shots, ranked by probability

**1. The Supply Nation trading test locks you out of the discovery surface. (HIGH)**
Registration requires **A$50,000 minimum annual revenue *or* demonstrated recent trade history,
ideally 6 months** [V]. Certification requires **A$50k revenue evidenced in the last 12 months**
[V1]. You are barely trading. If IBD is how buyers find Exemption 16 suppliers, and you are not
listed, **your single most valuable commercial asset is inert.** Resolve in one email.

**2. Exemption 16 removes the tender, not the delegate's personal risk. (HIGH)**
Direct-sourcing A$150,000 from a business with no past performance is a decision an ANAO
performance audit can land on. And ANAO **is** actively auditing this space — Report No. 40
2024-25 (June 2025) found Indigenous participation requirements **exempted from two-thirds of
eligible contracts (~A$70bn)**, only **one in five** subject contracts assessed for compliance,
**over a quarter of those non-compliant**, and NIAA guidance **not updated since July 2020** [V].
Buyers under audit scrutiny get *more* conservative, not less. **Your first contract must be
small enough to be career-safe** — which is exactly why the $80k–$200k band, not $500k, is the
realistic target.

**3. Anything with wages or inventory is fatal below A$50k capital. (HIGH, for those categories)**
Worked example, labour hire [ESTIMATE — inputs shown, attack them]: one contractor at A$1,200/day
× 20 days = **A$24,000/month billed**; at a 15% gross margin your cost is **~A$20,400/month**.
You pay the contractor fortnightly; the client pays on 20–30 day terms. Permanent float ≈ six
weeks of cost ≈ **A$28,000**. **Your entire capital base supports approximately one contractor.**
That returns ~A$43,000 gross margin on ~A$288,000 of revenue, before any overhead — a job, with
all your capital locked up and zero resilience to one late payment. Same arithmetic kills
cleaning, facilities management, security and reselling.

**4. The market is supply-saturated, not demand-starved. (HIGH)**
~6,000 verified businesses; ~1,200 won Commonwealth work last year (§5). The mandate created a
gold rush and the rush arrived. **Your differentiator has to be capability or relationship, not
status.** If your pitch is "we are Indigenous," you are the 6,000th person to say it this year.

**5. The 1 July 2026 eligibility change could disqualify you. (MEDIUM — structure-dependent)**
From 1 July 2026 the IPP requires **51%+ Indigenous owned *and controlled*** (or ORIC
registration), with FY2026-27 as a transition year accepting either test [V]. If your ownership
is exactly 50/50, or if effective control (board, casting vote, shareholder agreement) sits with
a non-Indigenous party, **you lose IPP eligibility at the end of FY2026-27.** Have a lawyer read
your constitution and shareholder agreement against the "formal ability to achieve a majority in
a general meeting" test [V]. This also means **the "Indigenous front for a non-Indigenous prime"
model is being deliberately closed** — do not build a plan around it.

**6. Panel channels are closed for the next 6–18 months. (MEDIUM)**
MAS admits no one outside a formal refresh; People Panel runs to June 2027; DMP2 closed Nov 2025
[V1]. If your plan depended on panel membership, the plan has no timeline. §6.1 is the answer,
but it rests on a single source — verify it or you have no channel at all.

## 10. Cheapest next test — 30 days, ~A$0

Run these four in this order. Three of them answer questions this memo could not.

**Test 1 — Eligibility (Week 1, one email, free). Resolves kill shot 1.**
Email Supply Nation: *"We are 51%+ Indigenous owned and controlled but pre-revenue. What
evidence of trade satisfies registration, and separately certification?"*
**Stop condition:** if you cannot be listed on IBD within 90 days by any route, **stop
everything else** and generate A$50k of any revenue first — private, state, or corporate. Without
an IBD listing the Commonwealth thesis does not function.

**Test 2 — Saturation and real prices (Week 1–2, free, one to two days' work).**
On **AusTender**, extract 24 months of contract notices in your 3 candidate categories: supplier
name, value, buying entity, procurement method. Cross-match suppliers against **IBD**. You will
get, for free, the four numbers this memo lacks: **how many Indigenous suppliers actually win in
your category, the median contract value, which agencies buy, and the named contact officers.**
Simultaneously count IBD listings per category.
**Stop condition:** if a category shows **>150 listed Indigenous businesses** and **<50
Commonwealth contracts per year**, that category is oversupplied — drop it and move to the next.

**Test 3 — Verify the flexibility framework (Week 2, free). Resolves kill shot 6.**
Read the **MAS Panel User Guide (July 2026)** and the **People Panel Phase 1/2/3 User Guides**,
and confirm the First Nations off-panel allowance in the actual text. Then email the Department
of Finance MAS/People Panel contact and ask them to confirm in writing.
**Stop condition:** if the off-panel First Nations allowance does not exist as described, you
have **no channel to panelled categories until the next refresh** — abandon rows 2, 6, 7 and 9
of §7 and concentrate entirely on non-panelled categories (rows 1, 3, 4, 5).

**Test 4 — Ask 20 buyers how they buy (Week 2–4, free). Resolves §3.4.**
Email 20 Commonwealth IPP/Indigenous procurement contact officers, one question:
*"When you want to use Exemption 16, how do you find a supplier?"* Do not pitch. Just ask.
Their answer tells you whether IBD listing, category tagging, or direct relationship is the
actual mechanism — and 20 emails to contract officers is the cheapest customer-discovery channel
in this entire market. **Expect 3–6 replies** [ESTIMATE].
**Stop condition:** if the dominant answer is *"we re-use suppliers we already know"*, then
incumbency is the moat, a cold new entrant cannot win the $80–200k band, and you must **enter as
a subcontractor to an established Indigenous business first** — a materially different plan.

### What "success" looks like at day 30

You will have: IBD listing status confirmed; a counted, priced, named list of who competes and
who buys in your top 3 categories; written confirmation of the off-panel route; and 3–6 buyers
who have replied to an email. **That is a qualified pipeline built for zero dollars** — which is
the correct first milestone for a business with <A$50k, not a website and a capability statement.

### On the "large outcome"

Say it plainly: **"cash within 12 months" and "a path to a large outcome" pull in opposite
directions here.** The categories that produce cash fast (rows 1, 3, 4) are the ones with the
lowest ceilings and the heaviest saturation. The categories with a ceiling (facilities
management, labour hire, ICT) need capital and staff you do not have. The JV-with-a-prime route
to scale is being **deliberately narrowed** by the 1 July 2026 owned-*and-controlled* test.

The only honest sequence is: **months 0–12, buy references with low-capital delivery work;
months 12–36, use the references plus retained cash to enter a category with a ceiling.** The
asset you are accumulating in year one is not revenue. It is the list of Commonwealth entities
that will act as a referee. Re-open the "large outcome" question at month 18 with real pipeline
data — not now, and not on the strength of this memo.
