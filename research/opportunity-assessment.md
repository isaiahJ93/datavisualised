# Opportunity assessment — where to actually start

**Question:** with Supply Nation registration, under A$50k, and barely trading — what makes
the most money, in any sector?

**Answer: nothing here makes you extremely rich. One thing here makes you money within
12 months at near-zero risk, and buys the position from which a larger move becomes
possible.** Seven research fronts, run independently across procurement, sectors, capital
flows, AI and export, converged on the same business. That convergence is the finding.

---

## 1. The gate — do this before anything else

On **1 July 2026** the Commonwealth definition of an Indigenous business changed from
**50% owned** to **51%+ owned AND controlled** (ORIC-registered corporations exempt).
Supply Nation **Registered** is the old 50% tier. **Certified** is the new one.

- Transitional cover runs through **FY2026-27** — eligibility ends around **1 July 2027**
- **Certification is free**, roughly **four-day** verification
- Victoria's Social Procurement Framework already requires certified status
- From 2026, every Certified Supplier faces a **mandatory face-to-face re-certification
  interview** verifying ownership, management and control

Three separate memos flagged this independently. **If the entity sits at exactly 50%, or
control is not demonstrable, every opportunity in this document expires on a known date.**
Get written confirmation from Supply Nation and NIAA before spending a dollar.

**Second gate, less obvious:** Supply Nation listing requires **A$50k minimum annual
revenue or ~6 months demonstrated trading**. If the business is not listed on Indigenous
Business Direct, buyers cannot find it — which makes the direct-award mechanisms below
inert. One email resolves this.

## 2. The business that survived all seven fronts

**Indigenous-owned bid writing, tender response and Aboriginal Participation Plan
authorship — sold as a service, not software.**

Two agents reached this independently from opposite directions. The low-capital analysis
ranked it **#1 of 11 niches** at roughly **10.7x gross profit per dollar of startup
capital**. The AI analysis ranked it **#1 of 8** as the only option with a durable moat.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Startup capital | **~A$6,000** |
| Licences required | **None** |
| Time to first revenue | **2-6 weeks** |
| Gross margin | **~85%** |
| Working capital per A$100k revenue | **~A$0 with deposits** |
| Cash CAC | **~A$0** — leads are free and public |

### Why it wins where everything else fails

**It inverts the working-capital trap that kills the alternatives.** The decisive
arithmetic: a labour-delivery business paying wages weekly against 30-day terms needs
**A$8,073 of standing float per A$100k of revenue**; at real-world 60-days-late,
**A$13,908**. With A$35k deployable that caps revenue at ~A$252k. **Buy a ute and sign kit
(~A$24k) and the ceiling drops to ~A$79k.** Buying delivery capability cuts the achievable
business by two-thirds — and it will feel like progress while it happens.

Fixed-fee advisory with a 50% deposit has **zero or negative** working capital. Channel and
invoicing terms are a ~7x lever, larger than any cost control.

**The buyer has already decided to spend.** A tender fee of A$4,000-8,000 is attached to a
contract worth 10-100x that. This is the exact opposite of the Vaultry compliance buyer,
who was asked to pay for an obligation with no upside.

**Indigenous ownership is a genuine qualification here, not a door-opener.** Writing an
Aboriginal Participation Plan is work the ownership legitimately strengthens. That matters
commercially and it matters for integrity: it is the one use of the status that cannot be
characterised as black cladding.

### Two buyers, and the second one is the business

**Buyer A — Indigenous businesses bidding for set-aside work.** 1,200+ won Commonwealth
contracts last year. Acute need, low internal bid capability. **But the average contract is
~A$123k**, so a firm winning two a year cannot fund a A$48k retainer. Sell only to Buyer A
and this is Vaultry in a new coat of paint.

**Buyer B — non-Indigenous civil and infrastructure primes.** They must evidence Indigenous
participation to win major public work, and someone must write that section. They pay from
an existing bid cost line, and **they cannot become Indigenous-owned to avoid needing you.**
~610 ANZ civil firms sit in the 20-199 employee band with real bid budgets.

**The business only works if Buyer B is at least half the book.**

### The numbers

```
Per-tender, solo:  ~115 delivery days/yr ÷ ~2.5 days per tender with AI leverage
                   = 35-45 tenders/yr at ~A$5,000
                   Year 1: A$90-125k     Year 2: A$175-225k

Retainer model:    A$3,000-6,000/mo "bid function as a service"
                   8 clients x A$4,000 = A$384k/yr
                   (beats per-tender because it removes per-engagement sales cost)

Ceiling:           ~670 serviceable firms x 2-4% attach = 13-27 clients
                   ~A$960k-1m/yr with 3-5 people
```

Market rates are established: **A$165 + GST/hr** (band A$150-350), **A$4,000-8,000 + GST**
per typical tender, **A$15,000-40,000** for majors. An in-house bid coordinator costs
~A$90-130k plus on-costs — that is the pitch anchor for the retainer.

**A retainer book is saleable. A per-tender pipeline is not.** Exit is 1-3x revenue to a
bid-consultancy roll-up.

### Distribution — 20 customers, not 100

**AusTender contract notices are free and public.** Every Indigenous business that won
Commonwealth work in the last 12 months is a named lead with a proven bidding habit and a
proven budget. Add Supply Nation Business Direct and Connect (buyers come to you), state
prequal registers, and direct approaches to bid managers at the civil primes. Near-zero cash
CAC is precisely why this fits under A$50k and Vaultry did not.

## 3. Where the mandates actually bite

The arbitrage is in **how a mandate is expressed**:

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| % of contract **value** | % of contract **count** |
| "employment **or** subcontracting" (buyer chooses) | **direct-award permissions** |

A A$25k contract earns the same "one contract" credit as a A$25m one. **Sell small, sell
direct, sell often.**

**The three doors** that let a business with no past performance win, because they are
limited approaches rather than open tenders:

- **Exemption 16, CPRs** — direct award to an Indigenous SME. *Nuance that matters:* it
  exempts you from the open-tender machinery, which only engages **above A$80,000**. Below
  that threshold a buyer can already directly engage anyone, so the exemption adds nothing.
  **Its value sits above A$80k.**
- **Mandatory Set-Aside** — first refusal: **any value in remote Australia**, and
  **A$80k-200k** elsewhere.
- **NSW direct engagement up to A$250,000**, permitted even where a mandated panel exists.

**The sweet spot is the A$80k-200k overlap.** A A$45,000 proposal throws away the best
asset in the business.

**Panel workaround, single-sourced — verify before relying on it:** the Management Advisory
Services and People panels reportedly run a Flexibility Framework letting agencies spend an
additional "A$50,000 or 5%, whichever is greater" to engage First Nations businesses
**off-panel**. Path: flexibility allowance → Exemption 16 → direct engagement. No panel, no
tender. If this does not exist, panelled categories are closed.

## 4. What was ruled out, and why

**The Gulf — hard no, stop spending attention on it.** Civil construction is not a tradeable
service; "exporting" it means standing up a licensed, bonded entity in-country. That is FDI.
Cost before first invoice: **~A$425-450k**. Aramco vendor registration requires **2-3 years
of audited financial statements** — a document that cannot be created faster than time
passes. Aramco's iktva hit **70% local content in Feb 2026**, targeting 75%: a supplier with
no Saudi payroll scores near zero and **damages the buying officer's own KPI**. The Line was
suspended Sept 2025 and scaled from 170km to ~2km, with a US$8bn PIF write-down. **CIMIC,
Australia's largest contractor, wrote off A$1.23bn on BIC Contracting and left the region.**

**Indigenous-to-Indigenous trade — a distraction.** IPETCA is non-binding and funds missions,
not contracts. Canada's PSIB requires owners be **resident in Canada**. NZ requires Māori
ownership. Only **1.5%** of First Nations businesses export at all, and those that do sell
culture, IP and product — never dirt.

**Health, care and financial services — no-go for 24 months.** Care is a working-capital and
compliance business and you have neither. APRA's **CPS 230** (from 1 July 2025) imposes
seven mandatory contract clauses on *material* service providers that a micro-business
cannot credibly sign; it doesn't touch non-material suppliers where diversity spend sits, so
it closed the high-value door permanently rather than the one you'd use. At a bank you fail
the financial-viability gate first: 2-3 years of statements.

**Construction AI software — zero. Do not enter.** Document Crunch raised US$32.5m, reached
400+ customers, and was acquired by Trimble (Apr 2026). **Three months later Procore shipped
contract review as one of five agents in its entry-level bundle.** The category leader's
whole product became a line item in a competitor's starter tier inside one quarter. And the
control experiment already ran: **Bidhive** (Brisbane, bid management software — precisely
this domain) reached ~US$210k revenue in 2023, *down* from US$233k in 2021, and was acquired
by Responsive in May 2025. Meanwhile the **service** it tried to automate still bills
A$4,000-8,000 per tender.

**Traffic control and labour hire — defer to year two, IBA-funded.** 30% gross margin at
A$70/hr, ~A$34k gross profit per controller annually, but A$8-14k working capital per A$100k
revenue and you need 4-5 heads to fund one supervisor. The market is consolidating and
distressed: Altus is ~5,500 staff after rolling up three competitors, and administrators
warn of further failures through 2026.

**Cultural heritage monitoring — not available to you.** Gated by *statutory Traditional
Owner standing*, not business ownership. No standing on that country, no work.

**The JV shortcut — the trap the reforms exist to catch.** Borrowing a non-Indigenous
partner's plant and track record is the textbook black-cladding structure. Supply Nation now
takes a **daily ASIC feed** for real-time audits and launched an integrity unit in Oct 2025.
Removal from the directory ends the business. The correct answer to "no plant" is **IBA
asset finance** — reportedly start-up loans to A$150k with up to 30% as grant, and working
capital assistance to A$200k with no repayments for 12 months. Verify directly.

## 5. The expectations correction

Three findings that between them dismantle the optimistic version:

**Indigenous status is a queue ticket, not a moat.** Supply Nation lists **6,000+ verified
Indigenous businesses**; just over **1,200** won any Commonwealth contract in FY2024-25.
Roughly **four in five won nothing.** You are not competing against non-Indigenous firms —
you are competing against Indigenous firms with a decade of past performance.

**It is a distribution problem, not a scarcity problem.** In the IPP's first eight years,
**50% of A$7bn went to 18 businesses**; 50% of contracts by count to **11 businesses**.
Canberra firms took 40% of contract count. Model yourself in the tail, not at the A$1.33m
mean.

**No evidence was found that tier-1s struggle to find Indigenous subcontractors.** It was
searched for directly. Worse, the mandates can usually be satisfied without you: the
Commonwealth minimum is "4% of workforce **or** 4% of contract value subcontracted" —
head contractor's choice — and NSW's APIC 1.5% counts employment, training and consultation.
TfNSW's requirement can be met by "spending on education, training or capability building."
**Every mandate can be satisfied without giving you a subcontract.** The Commonwealth is
also *hitting* its targets — FY2024-25 exceeded both volume and value — so no panicked buyer
exists.

## 6. The sequence

**Cash-in-12-months and a large outcome pull in opposite directions here.** Fast-cash
categories have the lowest ceilings; high-ceiling categories need capital and staff you
lack; the JV route to scale is being deliberately narrowed. The honest structure is
sequential, not simultaneous.

**Months 0-12 — buy references, not revenue.** Advisory delivery at 85% margins with no
working capital. Target A$90-125k. **Year one's asset is the referee list and the trading
history**, which is what unlocks Supply Nation certification standing, IBA finance, and
prequalification.

**Months 12-36 — convert references into a position with a ceiling.** Retainer book toward
A$384k. Use IBA finance — not a JV — if delivery capability is genuinely warranted by then.

**Month 18 — reopen the large-outcome question with real pipeline data**, not speculation.
The two routes worth examining then, neither researched here: the UK Social Value Model and
Canada's 5% Indigenous procurement target as export analogues for a *knowledge* service, and
a trade sale of a retainer book.

**Probability of a venture-scale outcome from this starting position: low single digits.**
That is not a reason to skip it. It is a reason to take the version that costs A$6,000
instead of A$50,000, and to keep the capital intact.

## 7. The 30-day test — build nothing

**Test 1 — the eligibility gate. A$0, 3 days. Before anything else.**
Written confirmation from Supply Nation and NIAA on the 51% owned-and-controlled test, plus
your Indigenous Business Direct listing status. Upgrade Registered → Certified.
**Stop if it fails and cannot be restructured — the entire thesis expires.**

**Test 2 — the budget test. A$0-500, 3 weeks.**
Twelve calls. Six Indigenous businesses pulled free from AusTender contract notices: *"what
did your last tender cost you in hours and cash, and who wrote it?"* Six prime bid managers:
*"who writes your Indigenous Participation Plan section, and what line does it come from?"*
**Stop if fewer than 3 of 12 name a dollar figure they already spend** — no budget line
means this is Vaultry again.

**Test 3 — the revenue test. A$300-1,000, 2 weeks. The one that matters.**
Not a landing page — **win one paid engagement.** A live tender response at A$2,000 fixed
plus A$3,000 on award, and a A$1,500 Participation Plan module.
**Stop at zero paid engagements from 20 qualified conversations.**

**Test 4 — the saturation check. A$0, 4 hours.**
Filter Indigenous Business Direct for **Certified** suppliers by state and category.
**More than 15 in a category means it is not scarce. Fewer than 5 with live demand is a go.**

**What not to do in 30 days:** no product code, no grant application, no landing page, no
domain purchase, no credentials. Each is a way of avoiding Test 3. DISP membership, ISO
27001, security clearances and NDIS registration would consume the entire A$50k and unlock
nothing until you have staff and past performance.

## 8. Confidence

Same constraint as the first round: **the network egress policy blocked every primary
source** — NIAA, Supply Nation, ABS, AusTender, Defence, Austrade, all vendor pricing pages.
Search worked; fetching did not. Every figure is search-synthesised, not read off the page.
Each memo grades its own claims; see `METHODOLOGY.md`.

**Verify before acting:**

- The exact transition date and terms of the 51% rule (memos disagree: already in force vs
  covered to 1 July 2027)
- Whether the MAS/People Panel off-panel First Nations flexibility allowance exists —
  single-sourced, and panelled categories depend on it
- IBA finance terms — loan limits, grant proportion, trading-history requirement
- Supply Nation certification fee and the minimum-trading test for directory listing
- Whether the 3% Indigenous participation clause actually sits in the Downer, Ventia and
  Service Stream base-services contracts

**Not found, and possibly not public:** any measurement of Australian bid-fee movement
2023→2026; whether traffic-management majors subcontract to Indigenous SMEs; corporate RAP
Indigenous spend by category. Do not let anyone fill these with plausible numbers.
